# B — Mining and quarrying

[All sectors](index.md) · [Cross-sector ecosystems](ecosystems.md) · [Source PDF](Global_Industry_Business_Software_AI_Atlas_2026.pdf#page=20)

**Coverage:** 5 divisions / 10 groups / 14 classes / 47 business examples. Source pp. 20–23.

## Scope

The main split is between artisanal or small extraction sites and industrial operators with geological models, fleets and continuous sensor data. Expensive equipment makes selected predictive workflows commercially plausible.

**Evidence scope in the atlas:** Large-operator cases demonstrate feasibility, not prevalence across all mines. Oil, gas and mineral subtypes inherit cautious analyst estimates. [S10](references.md#s10) [S06](references.md#s06) [S07](references.md#s07)

The D/A ranges and class-level AI examples below are analyst estimates or candidate applications. They are not measured adoption rates or proof of buyer demand.

#### Group 051 — Hard-coal mining

<a id="class-0510"></a>
##### 0510 — Hard-coal mining

**Maturity estimate:** D1-D4 / A0-A3; confidence: medium.

- **Businesses:** underground coal mines; open-cut coal mines; hard-coal preparation operations.
- **Software:** Geological models, mine planning, fleet dispatch, maintenance, safety and enterprise systems.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Equipment-failure prediction and condition monitoring.
- **Adoption context:** Large mines have connected operations; artisanal sites may be largely manual. AI concentrates in larger operators.
