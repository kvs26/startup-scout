# Global industry and business atlas

This knowledge base reorganizes the user-supplied, AI-generated *Global Industry & Business Atlas: Software + AI (2026)* into linked Markdown. It is a **navigation map for choosing buyer groups and search vocabulary**, not a list of validated startup ideas. The PDF states a research cutoff of 11 October 2026. Its class-level software/AI bands and many workflow examples are analyst estimates; this conversion checked completeness against the PDF but did not independently verify its market or adoption claims.

[Original PDF](Global_Industry_Business_Software_AI_Atlas_2026.pdf) · [Cross-sector ecosystems](ecosystems.md) · [Focused business profiles](subtypes.md) · [Atlas references](references.md)

## Read the map

The atlas uses the ISIC Rev. 5 hierarchy: **22 sectors → 87 divisions → 258 groups → 463 activity classes**. Each sector file includes its division/group hierarchy, all class profiles, source page span, and its relevant cross-sector links. The [72 finer business profiles](subtypes.md) appear in their parent sector files. An “industry” here is an economic activity; a business model, product, occupation or company is a different thing. A firm can operate in several classes.

**D** describes software maturity from D0 (mostly manual) to D4 (software-critical). **A** describes purposeful AI use from A0 (none established) to A4 (AI central to the product). A range such as D1–D3 / A0–A2 is an editorial span, not an adoption percentage or a statistical confidence interval. “Low” and “medium” are estimate-confidence labels, not evidence that any listed buyer needs a new product.

## How the idea scout should use this

1. Use varied sectors and [ecosystem routes](ecosystems.md) to identify specific activities, organization types, buyer roles and consumer audiences. Class examples supply search vocabulary, not proof of a business opportunity.
2. Investigate customer work, desired experiences and existing spending through original accounts, artifacts and current alternatives. The PDF’s D/A ranges are not evidence of actual use or demand.
3. Apply the scout's [discovery](../.agents/skills/startup-idea-scout/references/discovery.md) and [commercial assessment](../.agents/skills/startup-idea-scout/references/commercial-assessment.md) guides. An existing competitor is a comparison baseline; a researched reason to be chosen can concern price, value, experience or distribution without an unsolved functional gap. Keep the atlas's suggested applications separate from observed deployment.

For formal classification or current legal/regulatory decisions, verify the activity and code against the relevant official classification. The PDF’s [reference list](references.md) provides the original starting links; country taxonomies and editions may differ.

## Relationships and navigation

[Cross-sector ecosystems](ecosystems.md) connect activities such as software publishing, manufacturing, operation, professional services and distribution with exact class links. In each sector file, **Cross-sector connections** points back to those ecosystems and to other participating sectors. These are activity relationships from the PDF, not claims that a business opportunity exists at their intersection.
