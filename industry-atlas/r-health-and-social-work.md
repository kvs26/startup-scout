# R — Health and social work

[All sectors](index.md) · [Cross-sector ecosystems](ecosystems.md) · [Source PDF](Global_Industry_Business_Software_AI_Atlas_2026.pdf#page=113)

**Coverage:** 3 divisions / 9 groups / 11 class / 38 business examples. Source pp. 113–115.

## Scope

Hospitals, physician practices, allied health, residential care and social support have different systems and risks. A laboratory is not a useful maturity proxy for a small physiotherapy clinic.

**Evidence scope in the atlas:** US hospital predictive-AI evidence is strong within its scope. It must not be extrapolated to all countries, clinics or social-care providers. [S25](references.md#s25) [S26](references.md#s26) [S27](references.md#s27)

The D/A ranges and class-level AI examples below are analyst estimates or candidate applications. They are not measured adoption rates or proof of buyer demand.

## Activity catalogue

<a id="division-86"></a>
### Division 86 — Human health

#### Group 861 — Hospitals

<a id="class-8610"></a>
##### 8610 — Hospitals

**Maturity estimate:** D2-D4 / A1-A3; confidence: medium.

- **Businesses:** general hospitals; specialist hospitals; psychiatric hospitals; surgical hospitals.
- **Software:** Electronic health records, imaging, laboratory, pharmacy, billing and hospital operations systems.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Imaging support, note drafting and bed-demand forecasts.
- **Adoption context:** Digitally mature hospitals can deploy several AI tools; resource-limited hospitals may lack the foundations.
- **Watchpoint:** Validate clinical performance and retain clinician accountability.

#### Group 862 — Medical and dental practices

<a id="class-8620"></a>
##### 8620 — Medical and dental practices

**Maturity estimate:** D1-D3 / A0-A2; confidence: medium.

- **Businesses:** general practices; specialist clinics; dental practices; outpatient physician clinics.
- **Software:** Patient records, appointments, billing, prescriptions and clinical documents.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Note drafting, scheduling and reviewed clinical assistance.
- **Adoption context:** Large groups use more integrated tools than solo clinics; AI is often limited to documentation.
- **Watchpoint:** Protect patient data and review clinical outputs.

#### Group 869 — Other human health services

<a id="class-8691"></a>
##### 8691 — Health-service intermediation

**Maturity estimate:** D2-D4 / A0-A2; confidence: low.

- **Businesses:** doctor-booking marketplaces; diagnostic-service booking agents; health-provider matching services.
- **Software:** Provider directories, availability, booking, payments and patient communications.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Provider matching and administrative enquiry triage.
- **Adoption context:** Platforms can use matching and support tools without delivering clinical care themselves.
- **Watchpoint:** Do not confuse matching with diagnosis or quality guarantees.

<a id="class-8699"></a>
##### 8699 — Other human health services

**Maturity estimate:** D1-D3 / A0-A2; confidence: low.

- **Businesses:** physiotherapy clinics; diagnostic laboratories; ambulance services; allied-health practices.
- **Software:** Appointments, patient or sample records, billing and discipline-specific clinical systems.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Documentation, scheduling and specialist decision support.
- **Adoption context:** Laboratories and imaging centres can be more automated than small therapy practices.
- **Watchpoint:** Use discipline-specific validation and qualified clinical review.

### Division 87 — Residential care

#### Group 871 — Residential nursing care

<a id="class-8710"></a>
##### 8710 — Residential nursing care

**Maturity estimate:** D1-D3 / A0-A2; confidence: low.

- **Businesses:** nursing homes; skilled nursing facilities; residential rehabilitation nursing facilities.
- **Software:** Care plans, medication records, staffing, occupancy, billing and family communications.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Care documentation and staffing forecasts.
- **Adoption context:** Larger care groups have better data; AI is usually administrative or supervised monitoring.
- **Watchpoint:** Respect dignity, consent and clinical responsibility.

#### Group 872 — Residential mental-health and substance-use care

<a id="class-8720"></a>
##### 8720 — Residential mental-health and substance-use care

**Maturity estimate:** D1-D3 / A0-A1; confidence: low.

- **Businesses:** residential mental-health centres; substance-use rehabilitation residences; therapeutic residential communities.
- **Software:** Restricted care records, care plans, staffing, incidents and billing systems.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Administrative documentation with strict clinical oversight.
- **Adoption context:** Sensitive populations call for cautious, narrow AI use rather than autonomous care.
- **Watchpoint:** Strong consent, privacy and safeguarding are essential.

### Division 88 — Non-residential social work

#### Group 881 — Non-residential elder and disability social work

<a id="class-8810"></a>
##### 8810 — Non-residential elder and disability social work

**Maturity estimate:** D0-D3 / A0-A1; confidence: low.

- **Businesses:** home-help agencies; adult day-care centres; disability support services; elder social support organizations.
- **Software:** Case records, visit schedules, referrals, safeguarding and grant or service billing.
- **AI applications / candidates:** Visit scheduling and reviewed case summaries.
- **Adoption context:** Small providers may be partly paper-based; AI is usually limited assistance.
- **Watchpoint:** Never replace case-worker judgment or safeguarding duties.
