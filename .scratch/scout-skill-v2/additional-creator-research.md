## Finding and inspection limits

Twelve additional channel candidates offer useful coverage, with different evidence strength. The most immediately useful substantive sources are **Marc Lou, Nick Saraev, Jay Clouse and TK Kader**: their accessible originals contain specific operating problems, experiments or acquisition reasoning. **Nate Herk** adds a practical solo-automation lane; **Noah Kagan** adds customer/payment experiments. Nathan Barry supplies distribution-system leads. The remaining candidates are conditional, especially when only episode promotion or metadata was readable.

These are sources to sample according to the chosen business area, not twelve mandatory channels or endorsements. All earnings remain creator/guest claims, not verified results or expected user outcomes. Software, AI and similar technology remain the eligibility boundary. Broader business or creator examples can reveal technology workflows without making the underlying offline/education business eligible.

Inspection used current web search and original pages. No video was watched or listened to; no YouTube caption track was retrieved. One original publisher-hosted transcript was inspected in relevant excerpts (Jay Clouse). Other substantive observations come from creator-written material; episode summaries, profile copy and inaccessible video links are explicitly distinguished below. Search-engine publication estimates were not treated as reliable exact dates. Every link below was surfaced from originals or first-party search results; direct video failures do not imply the videos have been removed.

## Proposed method: emerging attention and routes to payment

This is a proposed scouting method, not a measured trend forecast or a new agreed quota. No candidate above was proved likely to go viral in coming months.

1. For a promising topic, collect dated original examples from a recent window appropriate to the brief (for example 30–90 days) and compare with older examples and the same channel's normal response. Record publication dates separately from page update dates. A current cumulative view count does not establish growth velocity; require repeated observations for that claim. If historical metrics are unavailable, say so.
2. Separate audience curiosity from buyer behavior: views, shares and creator comments are attention; identifiable operators describing an expensive workflow, paid alternatives, procurement and actual purchases are commercial signals. Seek independent operator evidence outside creator education funnels. Sample comments cautiously; anonymous comments are leads, not validated buyers.
3. Check saturation and copied stories. Group clips, reels, newsletters, interviews of the same business and reused demonstrations under the original business/episode. Look for independent adoption, viable differentiation and worsening acquisition economics. Ten creators teaching the same agency offer can indicate competition among sellers rather than rising buyer demand.
4. Propose a concrete, bounded distribution experiment for each serious idea: buyer, channel, why they would share/respond, offer, effort/cash budget, measurement period and what would change the decision. Connect the path from impression to relevant visit, activation/demo, paid pilot/purchase and repeat use. Report expenses and founder time, refunds/support and retention; high reach alone is insufficient.

Examples of **hypotheses to test later**, not actions taken: a small software tool could demonstrate one before/after workflow in a relevant community and measure qualified visitors converting at a displayed price; an AI service could use a narrowly scoped workflow case study to test paid-pilot interest with identifiable operators; a product producing a useful shareable output could test whether recipients become paying users, tracking referred cohorts separately. Marc's product/launch essays, Nick's account of audience changes, and TK's customer-profile article motivate these questions, but none establish a universal formula. Use truthful demonstrations and authorized channels; this research does not authorize publication or outreach.
