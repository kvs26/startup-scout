## Answer

Resolved 2026-10-02 through the live discussion with the user.

### What the suite must help answer

Every selected opportunity receives deeper investigation across the areas below. Each area gives specific findings, their sources, assumptions, evidence against the idea, and important unanswered questions. Missing information stays visible; filling every heading is not proof that the business will work.

| Area | Minimum useful questions |
| --- | --- |
| Customers and problem | Who has the problem, when does it happen, and what does it cost them in money, time, or missed outcomes? Who uses the product, who pays, and who can approve the purchase? These can be the same person or different people. |
| Alternatives and reasons to switch | What do people use now, including manual work or doing nothing? What might make them change, and what would make changing difficult? |
| Offer and income | What would we sell, to whom, at what price, for what unit or outcome, and with what payment terms? What supports that proposal? |
| Costs and cash | What would starting and delivering it cost? What work would the founder do? What repeats as usage grows, and when would money leave and arrive? |
| Finding customers and selling | Where could relevant customers be reached, why might that route work, and what steps would a purchase require? |
| Starting and continuing use | How would someone start using it and reach a useful result? What would make them keep using, renew, or buy again where appropriate? |
| Ability to deliver | What technology, data, integrations, access, specialist help, or other dependencies are needed? What is still uncertain about delivery? |

Add detail where the idea calls for it: for example, enterprise purchasing approvals, both sides of a marketplace, or AI usage costs. Do not force every business into subscription or repeat-purchase measures. The founder starts solo; expose effort and resource needs without silently excluding demanding opportunities. This coverage does not require one skill per area.

Scouting retains the lighter boundary established in [Decide what scouting establishes and what remains untested](04-decide-scout-boundary.md). It helps choose what deserves deeper investigation; it does not require a full business plan for every candidate.

### What deeper investigation helps the user decide

The investigator gives reasons for a possible next step, and the user chooses:

- **Keep exploring:** the idea deserves another step, such as a customer conversation or a focused test. This does not mean it is proven or ready to build.
- **Change it:** revise a particular part, such as the customer group, problem, offer, or price, because of what was learned. Preserve what changed and why.
- **Leave it:** consider setting the current idea or version aside, explaining the evidence and limits of that advice. This is not proof that every opportunity in that market is bad.

The investigation should explain what is known, what is uncertain, and which next action could change the decision. An unfinished customer question is a valid reason to pause a round and return with results; more website reading cannot substitute for those results.

### Numbers must have a clear basis

Use evidence about competitors for competitor prices, offers, and terms. Use relevant sources for other inputs, such as supplier prices for delivery costs. Record the source and relevant date, market, currency, and units; do not treat an advertised price as an observed sale or silently transfer it to another market.

Calculations may explore possible cases. Clearly distinguish sourced inputs, user-provided inputs, and hypothetical inputs used to ask “what if?” An imagined customer count is not a forecast; a competitor's price does not establish what customers will pay this business. When a value has no reasonable basis, say it is unknown and explain how it could be learned.

Separate initial and recurring costs, cash spending and unpaid founder effort, and revenue and cash collection. Explain which uncertain inputs change the conclusion. Avoid made-up precision or automatic rejection based on a simulated case.

### Investigation and customer connection work together

The investigator identifies the important unanswered customer questions and prepares suitable customer-test plans. Each plan says what needs to be learned, from whom, how to learn it, what to record, and which results would support, challenge, or leave the question unanswered. Explain likely effort or cost where there is a basis, keeping unknowns explicit. The test must fit the claim; a conversation about a problem does not by itself establish purchase or continued value.

For ideas the user chooses to advance, a separate skill helps find relevant people and available contact details with sources, draft tailored emails and follow-ups, prepare meetings, explain the offer, practise difficult conversations, and prepare pricing discussions and negotiations. It supports both learning from potential customers and later sales conversations. Its preparation uses the selected idea, offer, and investigation findings.

The user accepted this split. The new skill's name, detailed contract, and implementation timing remain with [Agree the startup skill suite blueprint](08-decide-suite-blueprint.md). This decision does not itself authorize sending messages, spending, or running a live test.

### Investigation is a repeatable loop with saved work

The agreed flow is: investigate an idea, prepare the next customer questions or tests, use the separate skill to help connect with people, bring the results back, and continue the investigation. Repeat as useful; the user decides whether to keep pursuing the idea.

Save the work for each opportunity across sessions: research notes and sources, findings and contrary evidence, assumptions, calculations and their inputs, test plans, supplied customer results, recommendations, and the user's decisions. Preserve earlier versions and why conclusions changed, alongside a clear current view. Saved work must live in durable project records rather than relying on chat memory.

On return, the investigator loads that work, identifies the new results and the claims they bear on, and updates the relevant findings and next steps. It must not start from scratch, silently discard conflicting results, count the same result twice, or present a planned test as completed. Distinguish what the user reports from any supporting artifacts supplied. New results may strengthen, weaken, or leave a claim unresolved; old findings can also need revisiting if their source or circumstances have changed.

This loop is a required part of the investigation skill's instructions and behavioral checks, not an optional future enhancement. The exact files and update rules belong to [Decide shared opportunity records and skill handoffs](06-decide-shared-records.md).

- Future first-customer work receives the customer and buyer findings, offer and terms, possible ways to reach people, customer-test plans, and any results already collected. A handoff does not mean those claims have all been confirmed.
- [Decide shared opportunity records and skill handoffs](06-decide-shared-records.md) sets the saved records and the return path for results, including the requested simple glossary.
