# Scout report prototype — revised after user feedback

**THROWAWAY PLANNING EXAMPLE. All products, findings, searches and usage scenarios are fictional. No real research or customer contact was performed.**

Start with [the revised comparison](opportunities/comparison.md). It is the only file the user needs to read for this discussion.

## Accepted presentation

1. **Explain each idea:** what the product would do, who would use it, and a concrete example of use. Keep imagined usage separate from evidence.
2. **Compare the same questions in a table:** problem evidence, reasons to buy, possible payer and payment model, customer access, delivery, costs and biggest doubt.
3. **Give a direct comparison in one block:** explain where one idea has a stronger basis or greater demands than another, why that changes the suggested next investigation, and what could change the recommendation. Do not leave the user to infer the differences from separate descriptions.
4. **Say what remains unfinished:** make important evidence gaps and older findings visible.
5. **Link optional detail:** the user should understand the ideas and comparison without reading the folders or research history.

The user said the original cards described separate points without comparing the ideas, and that the wording under “Problem and evidence” did not explain the product. They requested a direct comparison block. On 2026-10-06 they accepted the revised presentation above. The earlier proposal of six compressed lines per idea is superseded. The canonical decision is the [ticket resolution](../../issues/09-prototype-scout-output.md#answer).

Use ordinary phrases such as “existing billing software,” “who might pay,” and “what you would have to build and support.” Explain the actual work before describing evidence about it. Do not achieve brevity by removing the explanation needed to understand an unfamiliar idea.

All files stay beneath this planning prototype. Their nested paths illustrate future storage; they are not real opportunity records or implemented skills. Three examples are not a required candidate count. Detailed evidence remains saved once in each research file, with linked summaries.

## Review outcome

The user accepted the revised explanation and comparison with “yeah this sounds good.” The ticket is resolved; implementation belongs to the scout build task. No overall startup score, assumed country preference, or invented cost/profit comparison is introduced. These fictional assets illustrate the agreed presentation and do not establish real business findings.
