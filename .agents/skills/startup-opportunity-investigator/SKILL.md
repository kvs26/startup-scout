# Startup opportunity investigator

Help the user decide whether to keep exploring, change, or leave a chosen opportunity. Produce a sourced current view and the next useful customer tests; preserve each round so a later session can continue from files alone.

## Start or resume

Resolve the repository root from this skill's location (`../../..`). Read the shared [record guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/record-guide.md), [research guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/research-guide.md), and [tool routing](../../../docs/startup-skills/tools.md). Consult the single [source catalog](../../../docs/startup-skills/source-catalog.md) when choosing sources and go beyond it where the question requires. The root [glossary](../../../CONTEXT.md) owns domain terms. Runtime work needs neither the planning tracker nor the old project.

Outputs normally live at the repository root; an explicitly supplied output workspace changes that destination, not the shared-resource location. Resolve references and search existing summaries before creating an opportunity:

- **Scout handoff:** read the current summary, relevant scouting report, detailed evidence, calculations, tests, and history. The current opportunity governs findings; the old report supplies search context. Preserve the proposition, contrary evidence, unresolved claims, and the user's selection separately from the scout's recommendation.
- **Direct idea:** start or resume the same opportunity record without requiring a scout run. Retain the user's idea, customer and market. Missing initial evidence becomes research for this round, not a prerequisite the user must supply.
- **Returned results:** load the current opportunity, relevant detail, history and outstanding tests; then use the record guide's result intake for files or pasted notes with a project reference. Preserve original submissions and separate user reports from supporting artifacts. Check event identity before assigning a result ID; repeats add receipt history, corrections link the earlier account, and possible duplicates stay qualified. Use the saved records, not previous chat, to reassess affected claims.

Ask only when identity, scope, or a missing fact materially changes the work. Explain what can proceed while waiting. The founder starts solo; expose effort, capital, customer access and specialist dependencies. There is no default country, industry or subscription model, and unspecified time/budget/income preferences stay unknown. Software, AI and similar technology serving any industry are eligible; resource-intensive delivery is a finding to evaluate, not an automatic exclusion.

## Investigate what could change the decision

Cover the following areas with useful findings and explicit unknowns. Go deeper where an answer could change the next step; headings alone do not establish coverage.

| Area | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Customer and problem | Who experiences the problem, when, and with what consequences? Who uses, pays, and approves the purchase? What supports each role? |
| Alternatives and switching | What products, services, manual work or inaction serve the need now? Does the alleged gap still exist? What creates value beyond those alternatives, and what makes switching difficult? |
| Offer and income | What is being sold, to whom, for what unit/outcome and proposed price/payment terms? Which parts are assumptions? |
| Costs and cash | What is required initially and repeatedly, including usage costs, acquisition, support and unpaid founder work? When are costs paid and customer money collected? |
| Customer access and sale | Where could relevant customers be reached, why might that route work, and what buying steps or approvals are involved? |
| Starting and continuing use | How does a customer reach the first useful outcome? What could support continued value, renewal or another purchase where relevant? |
| Delivery | What technology, data rights/access, integrations, specialist work, operations and other dependencies are needed? What remains uncertain? |

Use original sources and useful follow-up, including current first-party product, price and supplier information. Investigate material contradictions, resolved complaints, satisfied users and cheaper alternatives. Keep claim-specific confidence and the strongest case against the offer visible. Public research, advertised features, supplied customer reports and assumptions retain their distinct meanings under the research guide. A customer conversation about a problem cannot establish purchase or continued value for this product.

Use the shared [economics template](../../../docs/startup-skills/templates/economics.md) when calculations help. Show formulas and distinguish sourced, user-supplied and hypothetical inputs, with dates, market, currency, units and material exclusions. Keep startup/recurring spend, unpaid work, revenue and cash collection separate. Explain sensitivity to consequential unknowns with useful alternative cases. Competitor prices describe competitors; a proposed price and imagined customer count create a scenario, not willingness-to-pay evidence or a forecast. Unpriced dependencies remain visible.

For returned evidence, update affected findings, calculations and recommendations against both old and new observations. State what changed, what did not, and why. Preserve contradictions; the latest submission does not automatically supersede a stronger account. A genuinely new event from the same participant can add information without becoming another independent customer. Refresh dated external claims only where needed for the decision.

## Design focused customer tests

Choose the few unresolved claims most likely to change the next decision. For each, use the shared [customer-test template](../../../docs/startup-skills/templates/customer-test.md) to prepare an actionable plan: claim and decision, relevant participants and roles, questions or procedure, material to show, what to record, interpretation of supporting/contrary/ambiguous results, and known effort/cost or access needs.

Fit the method to the evidence needed. Past-work questions or a workflow walkthrough can inform the problem and alternatives; purchase evidence requires an actual buying decision in context; continuing value requires later behavior over a meaningful usage cycle. These are proposed tests, with execution and results recorded separately. Explain test-specific decision criteria without inventing universal sample sizes or success percentages. Revise the next test when results change the important uncertainty; preserve the prior plan and reason for the revision.

Contact research for specific people, tailored messages and negotiation preparation belong to `startup-customer-connect`. Hand it the current opportunity, offer and selected test with their limits. If that skill is unavailable, save the usable handoff and say so; do not claim it ran. Actual results return through the common file-or-pasted-notes intake. This investigation authorizes no outreach, purchases or live customer tests.

## End a round and save it

Continue accessible desk research while a consequential question could still change the recommendation. A normal round can stop once the business view is usable and the next material uncertainty needs customer evidence, or further targeted research mainly repeats findings. Sparse web evidence is uncertainty, not proof of absent demand. If missing access, user input or a requested work limit prevents useful completion, save an explicitly incomplete round with the blocker and remaining checks.

Follow the record guide to save only useful files under `opportunities/<stable-name>/`: canonical research/results, calculations, test plans, short current summary, a dated round and change history. A round preserves the actual conclusions at that time, relevant calculation inputs/results and test design; links to mutable files alone cannot preserve the earlier version. Re-read destinations before updating, retain stable IDs, and preserve original submissions and earlier outputs.

Refresh the affected content in `opportunities/comparison.md`, dating the assessment and identifying other entries not reassessed. A single-opportunity update is not a new full comparison; direct entry with no other candidates may compare the offer with its current alternative. Keep earlier `scouting-runs/` reports intact. Follow the shared templates and check generated links.

Finish in everyday language with the product explained, important findings and contrary evidence, the recommendation and its reasons, what remains unknown, why the round stopped, and the next focused test or investigation. Link the current summary, saved round and useful handoff. The user chooses whether to continue, revise or leave; an agent recommendation never fills in their decision.
