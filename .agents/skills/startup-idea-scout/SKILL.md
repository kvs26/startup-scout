# Startup idea scout

Find a commercially worthwhile business for this founder, balancing earning potential, product quality, ambition and a realistic way to start. Own public discovery, competitor research and the commercial assessment. Customers may pay for productivity, convenience, entertainment, identity, status, hobbies or companionship. A socially important problem, scientific novelty and an unserved market are not entry requirements. Competition is a source of evidence and potential positioning; assess why our version could attract customers at workable costs.

For **open discovery, deeply research at least 50 distinct ideas before the final comparison and recommendation**. Fifty names, search snippets or brief screening notes do not meet this requirement. Follow the [depth and completion rules](references/discovery.md#what-counts-as-deep-research) for every counted idea. Recommend the strongest researched option, save it as an opportunity, and briefly present **3–5 other top ideas in chat**. If none merits a positive recommendation, still present the **top 3–5 researched options for discussion**, with strengths, doubts and what could change the assessment. The user may reasonably prefer an idea the scout would not choose.

This minimum applies to a new discovery campaign, including a search within a supplied theme. Asking for one recommendation does not reduce the research minimum. Assessing a specific supplied idea or comparing named saved ideas stays within that scope; an explicit request for a smaller research exercise overrides the default. A credible candidate is a reason to test a business, not a promise of profit. Public research cannot establish that nobody has tried an idea or that customers will buy ours.

## Orient and choose the path

Resolve the repository root from this skill's location (`../../..`). An explicit output workspace changes record destinations, not reference or profile locations. Read the shared [record guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/record-guide.md), [research evidence guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/research-guide.md) and [tool routing](../../../docs/startup-skills/tools.md). Use [CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md) for domain terms and the [source catalog](../../../docs/startup-skills/source-catalog.md) as a starting menu, following relevant sources beyond it.

Read and maintain the shared [founder profile](../../../FOUNDER-PROFILE.md) under the record guide's profile rules. The current request takes precedence over older preferences. Separate interests and reported experience from competence, customer access or demand. Explain time, budget, learning, specialist and support requirements; a demanding idea may remain eligible with its fit conditions explicit.

- **Open discovery:** read [discovery](references/discovery.md), then recent relevant `scouting-runs/` and recorded rejections. Use the industry atlas to build a diverse research pool, complete at least 50 individual deep assessments, and compare the completed pool. A new campaign needs fresh research; a prior screening is revisitable with a named different audience, offer, advantage or new evidence. Respect explicit user rejections.
- **Supplied idea:** preserve its customer, motive and proposed offer. Use the relevant research path in [discovery](references/discovery.md), without an unrelated diversity tour. Read [saved user leads](references/user-leads.md) when the request refers to the Instagram posts, skills packages or the bank-to-fund KYC example. These are leads, not an automatic selection or proof of demand.
- **Continue a search:** read its saved index and individual assessments; verify the completed count and resume unfinished research without restarting or counting duplicates. Use current opportunity records for newer findings and decisions. A request for another option from a completed campaign can use its researched pool with relevant freshness checks; it does not require inventing another 50.
- **Continue a selected idea:** deeper business investigation belongs to `startup-opportunity-investigator`. Carry the current opportunity path and unfinished questions; if that skill cannot run, provide a usable handoff and say so.

Ask only when a missing answer materially changes scope or resolves an ambiguous record. Continue independent work while waiting. Tell the user where research is starting and give updates on findings and the next decision-changing check. Scouting authorizes no outreach, purchases or live customer experiments.

## Investigate the commercial case

Use [commercial assessment](references/commercial-assessment.md) for every candidate: reasons to choose it, acquisition, payment, repeat value, costs, founder fit and the decision state. Use the shared research guide for source provenance and claim-level uncertainty. A missing-feature complaint is one entry path; positive use, existing spending and desired experiences are others. Read original material and current alternatives at the actual customer tier. An attractive category or a revenue screenshot alone does not support our proposed business.

Reconstruct the customer's work or experience from trigger to outcome, then explain the offer in that context. Compare the strongest realistic substitutes, including free options, services and doing nothing. Distinguish a documented feature from demonstrated suitability, a listed price from a completed purchase, and category demand from demand for our version. An advantage can be a testable hypothesis grounded in research; accepted pricing, switching and retention usually remain customer-test questions.

Pursue accessible checks that could change each assessment. When progress stalls, change the evidence channel, audience or commercial angle. Keep source-access failures separate from findings against a business. For open discovery, reaching a promising early idea or rejecting the first few does not finish the campaign. If interrupted or unable to continue, save an explicitly incomplete checkpoint with the actual completed count, obstacles and next work; never claim the 50-idea search or its final comparison is finished. Use the commercial guide's [comparison and decision rules](references/commercial-assessment.md#compare-the-researched-pool) after the required research is complete.

## Explain and save

Save the campaign index, full comparison and final result in `scouting-runs/<run-id>.md` using the [report template](assets/scouting-report.md). Save each deep assessment in `scouting-runs/<run-id>/ideas/<ID>-<name>.md` using the [idea-assessment template](assets/idea-assessment.md). These files preserve research even for ideas not recommended. Use stable IDs, distinguish research completion from business verdict, and link evidence to its canonical home. Automatically promote only the strongest recommended idea into `opportunities/<stable-name>/research.md` and `summary.md`, referring to its existing evidence rather than copying observations; reuse its existing folder if already recorded. Runners-up and fallback discussion options stay in scouting records unless the user selects one. A supplied idea may have its own record even with a negative verdict. Preserve historical outputs and reread destinations before updating them. The campaign comparison is required; a separate comparison of existing opportunity folders follows the shared record guide when requested.

For the main recommendation or a supplied idea, write a self-contained explanation using the shared [explanation guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/explaining-ideas.md) and [summary template](../../../docs/startup-skills/templates/opportunity-summary.md), adapting work/problem language to the actual customer motive. Explain:

1. Who the customer is, what they want, what they use now, and why they spend time or money on it.
2. What we would sell, one clearly imagined use from input to result, what the initial version includes, and a plausible path to a larger business with its assumptions visible.
3. Who pays, what payment buys, and why purchase, renewal, repeated use or another sale might occur.
4. The strongest support and contrary finding, the strongest alternative, and our proposed reason to be chosen. Label untested advantages.
5. A concrete acquisition route: where the relevant people are, how the offer reaches them, and what access or cost is untested.
6. What building and running it requires, including human work, data, integrations, quality checks, usage costs, acquisition and support. Show assumptions and formulas for numerical scenarios.
7. Founder fit and **Domain knowledge needed**, following [domain learning](references/domain-learning.md). Cover the knowledge needed for this product, including consumer experience or distribution where relevant; scale depth to the task.
8. The recommendation, strongest reason for and against, next decisive unknown, research limits and why the round stopped.

The user should understand the business and their obligations without opening another file. Complete the explanation guide's comprehension check. Keep recommendations separate from user selection; avoid overall startup scores and unsupported profit rankings. Link detailed evidence and the dated run as optional reading.

Finish a completed discovery campaign with the strongest recommendation and its opportunity link, plus brief chat summaries of 3–5 other top ideas; if none qualifies, present the top 3–5 discussion options. Follow the commercial guide's [presentation rules](references/commercial-assessment.md#compare-the-researched-pool), including why they lead the pool, decisive doubts, completed count, saved comparison and next choice. An incomplete campaign ends only with a checkpoint and explicitly provisional findings. The user chooses whether to advance. A handoff carries the current proposition, evidence, contrary findings, assumptions, acquisition and delivery requirements, and unfinished questions. A handoff does not claim the investigator ran or customer validation happened.
