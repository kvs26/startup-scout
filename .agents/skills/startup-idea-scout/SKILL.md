# Startup idea scout

Find a commercially worthwhile business for this founder, balancing earning potential, product quality, ambition and a realistic way to start. Own public discovery, competitor research and the commercial assessment. Customers may pay for productivity, convenience, entertainment, identity, status, hobbies or companionship. A socially important problem, scientific novelty and an unserved market are not entry requirements. Competition is a source of evidence and potential positioning; assess why our version could attract customers at workable costs.

When the user chooses **small products aimed at earning quickly**, make the shortest credible path to first revenue the **top comparison priority**. This means products the founder owns, including narrow tools, playful/novelty apps and products built around an emerging trend; “gig” or freelancing does not describe this track. Read [small products and speed to revenue](references/commercial-assessment.md#small-products-and-speed-to-revenue) for payment timing, distribution, costs and viral hypotheses. Longer-term startup searches can prioritize durability and growth over the earliest sale; use the selected track rather than applying one priority to every search.

Include **Freelancing / paid client work** as a separate selectable category alongside repeatable services/agencies, small owned products and longer-term startups. An explicit “all categories” choice includes client work as well as owned products; a narrower choice retains its scope. Read [freelancing and paid client work](references/commercial-assessment.md#freelancing-and-paid-client-work) when this category is selected. Keep business model and potential scale separate: a freelancer can later build a repeatable service or product, but that growth is a hypothesis.

For **open discovery, deeply research at least 50 distinct ideas before the final comparison and recommendation**. Fifty names, search snippets or brief screening notes do not meet this requirement. Follow the [depth and completion rules](references/discovery.md#what-counts-as-deep-research) for every counted idea. Recommend the strongest researched option, save it as an opportunity, and briefly present **3–5 other top ideas in chat**. If none merits a positive recommendation, still present the **top 3–5 researched options for discussion**, with strengths, doubts and what could change the assessment. The user may reasonably prefer an idea the scout would not choose.

This minimum applies to a new discovery campaign, including a search within a supplied theme. Asking for one recommendation does not reduce the research minimum. Assessing a specific supplied idea or comparing named saved ideas stays within that scope; an explicit request for a smaller research exercise overrides the default. A credible candidate is a reason to test a business, not a promise of profit. Public research cannot establish that nobody has tried an idea or that customers will buy ours.

## Orient and choose the path

Read the root [skill map](../../../SKILL-MAP.md) at the start of each run for related project skills, routing and next-step guidance.

Resolve the repository root from this skill's location (`../../..`). An explicit output workspace changes record destinations, not reference or profile locations. Read the shared [record guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/record-guide.md), [research evidence guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/research-guide.md) and [tool routing](../../../docs/startup-skills/tools.md). Use [CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md) for domain terms and the [source catalog](../../../docs/startup-skills/source-catalog.md) as a starting menu, following relevant sources beyond it.

Read and maintain the shared [founder profile](../../../FOUNDER-PROFILE.md) under the record guide's profile rules. The current request takes precedence over older preferences. Separate interests and reported experience from competence, customer access or demand. Explain time, budget, learning, specialist and support requirements; a demanding idea may remain eligible with its fit conditions explicit.

Treat the founder's planned AI-assisted production through separate skills or automations as context for assessing feasibility, costs and founder fit. Follow the shared [skill boundaries](../../../docs/startup-skills/record-guide.md#skill-boundaries): this skill researches and recommends opportunities; it does not create code, art or products. Use [AI-assisted production](references/commercial-assessment.md#ai-assisted-production) to assess the plan, keeping proposed production checks separate from work performed.

- **Open discovery:** read [discovery](references/discovery.md), [saved user leads](references/user-leads.md), recent relevant `scouting-runs/` and recorded rejections. Establish the [opening brief](references/discovery.md#establish-the-opening-brief) with single-choice menus for missing answers, including actual industry/lead options; wait for selections or explicit delegation. Then use [category research assistants](references/delegation.md) for source discovery, assigned deep assessments and focused challenges, with the coordinator integrating one campaign. Complete at least 50 distinct deep assessments across the chosen scope before comparison. A new campaign needs fresh research; revisit a prior screening with a materially different proposition or new evidence. Respect explicit user rejections.
- **Supplied idea:** preserve its customer, motive and proposed offer. Use the relevant research path in [discovery](references/discovery.md), without an unrelated diversity tour. Read [saved user leads](references/user-leads.md) when the request refers to a saved idea or problem. Adding a lead to that notebook records it for future exploration; it does not start a scouting run or select it for investigation.
- **Continue a search:** read its saved brief, index and assessment sections; show the brief and resume without repeating opening menus. Reconcile changed constraints and verify the accepted count. Use [delegation recovery](references/delegation.md#failures-and-resumption) for unfinished assignments; continue in the same run without counting duplicates or stale worker claims. Older multi-file runs remain readable. Use current opportunity records for newer findings and decisions. Another option from a completed campaign uses its pool with relevant freshness checks; it does not require another 50.
- **Continue a selected idea:** deeper business investigation belongs to `startup-opportunity-investigator`. Carry the current opportunity path and unfinished questions; if that skill cannot run, provide a usable handoff and say so.

Ask only for missing brief choices or consequential ambiguity. While waiting for scope, prepare local menus and record known context; begin candidate research after actual selections/delegation. Tell the user where research is starting and update findings and the next decision-changing check. Scouting authorizes no outreach, purchases or live customer experiments.

## Investigate the commercial case

Use [commercial assessment](references/commercial-assessment.md) for every candidate: reasons to choose it, acquisition, payment, repeat value, costs, founder fit and the decision state. Use the shared research guide for source provenance and claim-level uncertainty. A missing-feature complaint is one entry path; positive use, existing spending and desired experiences are others. Read original material and current alternatives at the actual customer tier. An attractive category or a revenue screenshot alone does not support our proposed business.

Reconstruct the customer's work or experience from trigger to outcome, then explain the offer in that context. Compare the strongest realistic substitutes, including free options, services and doing nothing. Distinguish a documented feature from demonstrated suitability, a listed price from a completed purchase, and category demand from demand for our version. An advantage can be a testable hypothesis grounded in research; accepted pricing, switching and retention usually remain customer-test questions.

Pursue accessible checks that could change each assessment. When progress stalls, change the evidence channel, audience or commercial angle. Keep source-access failures separate from findings against a business. For open discovery, reaching a promising early idea or rejecting the first few does not finish the campaign. If interrupted or unable to continue, save an explicitly incomplete checkpoint with the actual completed count, obstacles and next work; never claim the 50-idea search or its final comparison is finished. Use the commercial guide's [comparison and decision rules](references/commercial-assessment.md#compare-the-researched-pool) after the required research is complete.

## Explain and save

Save **one Markdown file per scouting session/run**, `scouting-runs/<run-id>.md`, using the [report template](assets/scouting-report.md). Keep the index, full comparison, recommendation, all deep assessments, evidence, shared costs and resume notes inside it. Embed the [idea-assessment template](assets/idea-assessment.md) as repeatable sections, with stable idea/evidence anchors under the shared record guide. Keep the full research depth for every counted idea; the file limit changes storage, not the 50-idea requirement. Use temporary working storage if needed, then integrate it into the run; create no persistent per-idea files, batch dossiers or sidecar research folders. A continuation updates the existing run.

Automatically promote only the strongest recommended idea into `opportunities/<stable-name>/research.md` and `summary.md`, linking the run's evidence anchors rather than copying observations; reuse its existing folder if already recorded. This separate winning-opportunity handoff remains part of the workflow. Runners-up and fallback discussion options stay as sections in the run unless the user selects one. A supplied idea may have its own opportunity record even with a negative verdict. Preserve historical outputs and reread destinations before updating them. The campaign comparison is required; a separate comparison of existing opportunity folders follows the shared record guide when requested.

Use the shared [explanation guide](../../../docs/startup-skills/explaining-ideas.md) and [summary template](../../../docs/startup-skills/templates/opportunity-summary.md) for the saved business explanation. Keep opening choices separate from results. Present completed discovery results in this simple order, using short sentences and everyday language:

1. **The best idea:** what we would sell, who buys and why. Show one clearly imagined use, the current alternative and our proposed difference; distinguish starting model from possible scale.
2. **How you could earn:** first customer route, what payment buys, plausible price and costs with their uncertainty. Explain payment timing when it drives the chosen priority.
3. **What it takes:** the founder's work, learning and time, including delivery/support obligations and consequential dependencies. Use [domain learning](references/domain-learning.md) to explain what must be learned.
4. **Why it leads:** strongest support, biggest doubts, contrary findings and what remains unknown. Mention source gaps when they affect the recommendation.
5. **3–5 alternatives:** briefly explain each offer, buyer, appeal and main doubt.
6. **The next step:** what to investigate before building, with the handoff below.

Keep detailed source contributions, calculations and research trails in the linked run. Material uncertainty stays visible in chat. For a supplied idea, explain that idea without manufacturing alternatives; for incomplete or no-winner campaigns, apply the reporting rules below rather than claim a best idea.

The user should understand the business and their obligations without opening another file. Complete the explanation guide's comprehension check. Keep recommendations separate from user selection; avoid overall startup scores and unsupported profit rankings. Link detailed evidence and the dated run as optional reading.

Finish a completed discovery campaign with the strongest recommendation and its opportunity link, plus brief chat summaries of 3–5 other top ideas; if none qualifies, present the top 3–5 discussion options. Follow the commercial guide's [presentation rules](references/commercial-assessment.md#compare-the-researched-pool), including why they lead the pool, decisive doubts, completed count, saved comparison and next choice. An incomplete campaign ends only with a checkpoint and explicitly provisional findings. The user chooses whether to advance. A handoff carries the current proposition, evidence, contrary findings, assumptions, acquisition and delivery requirements, and unfinished questions. A handoff does not claim the investigator ran or customer validation happened.

Always finish with the skill map's [concrete next plan](../../../SKILL-MAP.md#explain-the-next-step), both in chat and in the run. For a completed recommendation, normally name `startup-opportunity-investigator`, the opportunity to carry forward, the next questions and a ready-to-use prompt; keep a proposed choice distinct from the user's selection.
