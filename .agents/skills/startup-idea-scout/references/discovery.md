# Discovery across buyers, motives and markets

Read for open discovery and continuation, or use the relevant per-idea method for a supplied idea. [Commercial assessment](commercial-assessment.md) owns business verdicts and comparison; the shared [research guide](../../../../docs/startup-skills/research-guide.md) owns evidence interpretation.

## Build a diverse research pool

A completed open-discovery campaign contains **at least 50 distinct, deeply researched ideas**, followed by a comparison. This is a research minimum, not a requirement for 50 positive verdicts or 50 pitches in chat. Early screening helps decide what to research; it cannot substitute for the 50 deep assessments.

Use the founder profile and recent runs to notice concentration by **audience, buying motive and business model**, not just industry names. Compliance software sold to several industries remains a concentrated search. For unrestricted discovery, cover unrelated sectors, discretionary consumer spending, non-regulated business work, existing paid categories and novel opportunities where promising. Record the distribution of completed ideas across these dimensions and explain concentration. A specific search theme narrows the eligible universe, not the research depth; a specific supplied idea needs only its own assessment.

Use the [industry atlas](../../../../industry-atlas/index.md) actively: follow relevant sector/class or [ecosystem](../../../../industry-atlas/ecosystems.md) links, extract activity names, customer roles and search vocabulary, then investigate current evidence outside the atlas. Its AI-generated D/A bands are navigation aids, not demand estimates or rankings. For consumer products, add audience and spending occasions such as fun, status or belonging. Record the atlas routes actually consulted.

An idea is a distinct commercial proposition: a particular audience, offer, buying reason and route to market. Renaming a tool, changing a price or adding a feature does not create another counted idea. A different segment or package counts separately only when it changes the commercial case and receives its own substantive research. Record related ideas and explain the distinction. Previously discarded duplicates, obviously out-of-scope concepts and quick rejections remain screening notes outside the completed count. Respect explicit user rejections.

Use starting points such as:

| Starting point | What to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Existing paid product or service | Who buys, what customers like, current pricing and acquisition; how another provider might win through cost, value, package, audience or experience |
| Consumer desire or habit | Entertainment, creation, identity, status, relationships, hobbies or convenience; first use, spending and reasons to return |
| Business work | A recurring task from trigger through people, systems and handoffs to outcome; paid services and internal work as well as software |
| Expertise, tools or distribution | Skills, templates, integrations, software-enabled services or an audience; the actual deliverable, buyer, maintenance and acquisition |

These are lenses, not a closed list. Social posts, launches and investor themes supply leads. Follow their claims into original evidence. Read relevant carousel slides as well as captions when posts are supplied. A large population does not establish reachable customers, but the existence of a competitor does not exhaust demand: examine new buyers, additional purchases, segments, tastes, service and distribution as well as switching.

## What counts as deep research

Give each idea a stable ID and a self-contained [assessment](../assets/idea-assessment.md). Work through all of the following with idea-specific findings, supporting and contrary sources, and explicit uncertainty:

1. **Customer and motive:** a specific audience, market and use/purchase occasion; actual work or desired experience; who uses, pays and approves. Reconstruct the sequence from trigger to result, separating observed and inferred steps.
2. **Demand or spending context:** original customer accounts, behavior, work artifacts or attributable spending evidence. Inspect source context, dates and relevant replies/resolutions. Positive use counts; a complaint is optional. Vendor assertions alone do not establish demand.
3. **Current alternatives:** investigate the strongest relevant paid, free, manual or service substitutes. Check current capabilities, pricing/terms and customer tier, including what customers like. Explain adoption or switching effort and whether a new product could be an additional purchase.
4. **Our offer and buying reason:** a concrete first version and imagined use; the parameter on which it could compete; the supporting evidence, untested assumption, and observation that could disprove it. “Already exists” is not a sufficient verdict.
5. **Acquisition and payment:** one concrete route to the intended audience, access and selling requirements, payment unit and why repeat use or another sale might occur. A public listing or channel is not a customer relationship.
6. **Economics and operations:** startup, fixed and usage costs, acquisition, support and founder labor; data, integrations and quality checks. Check accessible material supplier costs. Use sourced inputs or clearly hypothetical calculations and expose unpriced dependencies rather than inventing profit.
7. **Founder fit and ambition:** learning, domain judgment, time, budget, access and specialist requirements; potential profit growth and constraints beyond the initial version. Explain the mechanism and assumptions behind upside, not just a large market.
8. **Challenge and decision:** strongest reasons for and against, confidence by claim, the next decisive observation and a reasoned business verdict. Pursue accessible follow-ups that could reverse it before declaring the assessment complete.

A completed assessment integrates these findings into an understandable business case. Filling headings with generic prose, attaching unread links, repeating one promotional source, or leaving accessible core checks undone does not meet the depth standard. Research must address customer behavior, current supply and the entry/delivery case through appropriate sources; a link quota is not a substitute.

**Research completion and business quality are separate.** A deeply researched negative verdict counts. Private purchase acceptance, retention or costs may remain unknown after substantive desk research; document what was checked, why those facts remain unavailable and what direct observation would resolve them. This can be a complete assessment with an uncertain business verdict. A source-access obstacle with no substantive alternative evidence, or research not yet performed, remains incomplete and does not count. Research additional eligible ideas or resolve the obstacle to reach the minimum; do not pad the count.

## Keep a resumable campaign

Use `scouting-runs/<run-id>.md` as the index, comparison and progress record. Store individual assessments under `scouting-runs/<run-id>/ideas/`. Track stable IDs, related/duplicate IDs, assessment paths, research status, business verdict, latest check and remaining work. Keep source observations in one canonical file with stable evidence IDs; other ideas and the comparison link to it.

Research in manageable batches while preserving the same depth standard across the pool. After each batch, update the actual completed count and give a short progress update: completed N of at least 50, sectors/motives covered, changed findings and next work. Continue until at least 50 distinct assessments pass the depth check. An attractive first result is not a stopping condition.

Before final comparison, audit every counted file for the depth requirements, working evidence links, freshness and distinctness. Downgrade incomplete files and finish or replace them. Read the individual conclusions and their evidence; a table of unchecked labels is not a comparison.

If runtime, context, tool access or an explicit work limit prevents finishing, save a **research incomplete** checkpoint with the honest count and precise resume tasks. Keep useful provisional leaders visible, but do not describe them as the best of a completed 50-idea search. Resume from the index and current assessments, retaining completed work and correcting counts when necessary. Do not silently lower the target or claim work continued after the turn ended. An in-progress index can be updated; preserve completed reports and later changes under the shared record guide.

## Compare and report

After the minimum is met, apply the commercial guide's [comparison and presentation rules](commercial-assessment.md#compare-the-researched-pool) to all completed ideas. Save the full comparison, promote the strongest recommended idea into its opportunity folder and briefly mention 3–5 other top ideas in chat. If none merits a positive recommendation, present 3–5 strongest researched discussion options with their appeal, weaknesses and next checks. Other ideas remain in scouting records unless selected by the user. The founder's disagreement is a reason to explore their choice, not to hide it or automatically change the evidence verdict.

For a specific supplied idea, use the same per-idea depth and business judgment without generating an unrelated pool. For a comparison of named saved ideas, use their latest records and state any gaps. Only an explicit reduction in research scope changes the 50-idea default for open discovery.

## Recover from another empty round

Inspect whether ideas were dismissed because they had competitors, lacked complaints, were unfamiliar, or needed private buying evidence that research cannot supply. Correct those errors and pursue remaining accessible checks. If none is strong enough after the completed comparison, diagnose the obstacle while still presenting the top 3–5 options. A negative scout verdict does not veto the user's choice to investigate a lawful idea. Keep substantive commercial objections visible and avoid claiming either universal market exhaustion or guaranteed room for another profitable entrant.
