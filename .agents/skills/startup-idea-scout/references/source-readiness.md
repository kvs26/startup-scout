# Establish source access before researching candidates

Read for every scouting mode after establishing scope and **before candidate discovery, assessment or delegation**. On resumption, reconcile new links and changed requirements before continuing. Access checks are preparation; they do not authorize a campaign while its inputs remain unclear.

## Match sources to the chosen search

Translate the accepted category, audience, discovery angle, geography and supplied references into concrete source obligations in the run. The [source catalog](../../../../docs/startup-skills/source-catalog.md) is a starting menu; the brief determines what must actually be explored. Include relevant sources beyond that menu. State the reason each source family is required or optional, what question it answers, the material needed and its owner. Infer the research method from the established brief without an extra approval round.

Examples to adapt to the actual brief:

| Chosen search | Sources to explore and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Freelancing / paid client work | Current buyer briefs, relevant job/project marketplaces and practitioner/customer communities for requested deliverables, rates, buying process and delivery problems; creator tutorials alone cannot establish paid demand. |
| Repeatable services / agency | Customer workflows and buying requests, provider offers/prices, operator accounts and customer feedback for repeatability, acquisition and labor. |
| Small owned products | Actual products/stores, customer use/reviews and spending context, maker economics and relevant acquisition channels for first-sale timing and costs. |
| Consumer novelty, creator-led products or viral/emerging consumer trends | Current YouTube and Instagram originals where these channels serve the selected audience, including relevant Shorts/Reels/carousels, creator and audience context, plus independent product/customer checks. Explicitly scoped-out platforms may be omitted. Historical business interviews alone cannot establish what is spreading now. |
| Longer-term startups / a specialist industry | The industry's own practitioners, workflows, procurement/spending, current alternatives and relevant technical/market changes. Generic founder content cannot replace industry evidence. |
| Overseas-to-India adaptation | Original overseas offer/customer evidence plus Indian users, local alternatives, prices/payments, language and distribution. |

Use all applicable rows for a mixed brief. A platform the user explicitly asks to explore is required regardless of its default catalog role. A platform can be optional only when omitting it leaves the actual research question answerable; ease of access is not a reason to downgrade it. No fixed source quota or exhaustive reading of every site is required.

## Check supplied material and required routes

1. Inventory every link or attachment supplied for interpretation, evaluation or inspiration. Inspect the material needed to understand why it matters: relevant carousel slides and caption; reel/video speech and visual demonstration; linked text and context. Record the original URL, what was inspected, omissions, dates and provenance. A URL, thumbnail, search snippet or previous run's notes does not count as fresh inspection. Saved notes may be reused as dated evidence only when sufficient for the current question; they do not make an unread new link understood.
2. Check a usable route for each required source family before dispatching candidate research. Use available connectors, permitted browser access or original text/media appropriate to the claim. Follow the [creator access routes](../../../../docs/startup-skills/source-catalog.md#creator-access-routes) when YouTube/Instagram is required. Distinguish a documented capability, an account/configuration and a successfully inspected sample. Metadata retrieval alone does not prove content access. One successful sample establishes a route, not completed category coverage.
3. If supplied material is unread/partial or its intended role is consequentially unclear, **tell the user immediately, before starting candidate research**. Name and link the exact item, state what could and could not be read, why it matters, and request the missing material or clarification. For a carousel request the relevant slides and caption; for a reel/video request a transcript and relevant frames or a screen recording when motion/audio matters. Request uploads in normal chat, not a text-only question tool. Use known timestamps/slide numbers only; never invent them. The user can supply material, clarify that the link is optional, or explicitly choose a reduced scope.
4. If required platform/source access fails, disclose that failure at the same point. Pause candidate research until access/material is sufficient or the user explicitly changes scope. Continue only independent preparation, such as saving the brief, checking access or arranging inputs. An optional-source failure can be recorded and bypassed without a permission round. A new required gap found mid-run pauses the affected work immediately; genuinely independent work can continue if the established brief remains clear. If the gap affects the campaign's scope or comparison, pause the campaign.

The readiness check passes only when the brief is clear, supplied material is understood or explicitly made optional by the user, and required source families have usable routes. Silence, tool failure, earlier broad permission to research and permission to choose industries are not permission to drop a source requirement. Record any explicit scope change with its date and effect. Describe the narrow limitation without repeatedly asking about choices already settled.

Prefer available free/included retrieval. If obtaining required content would need a new paid plan, unapproved charges or spending beyond the authorized budget, ask the user for the transcript or missing visual material instead of purchasing access. The user explicitly prefers supplying material when retrieval is costly. Honor any paid route already specifically authorized; a planning cap alone is not that authorization.

## Carry obligations through research and completion

Pass the source obligations and access results to every worker. Workers report a required-source failure as soon as discovered; they cannot silently substitute unrelated sources or mark the assignment complete. The coordinator owns disclosure and the pause/clarification above. A substitute must answer the same question with sufficient provenance and modality; changing an explicit platform requirement needs the user's scope change.

Keep planned coverage separate from actual evidence. For each required family and supplied item, record **inspected / partial / unread / searched with no relevant results**, original evidence links, queries or selection method, publication and inspection dates, content modality, limitations, and remaining work. Record explicit user exclusions separately. Attempting a URL is not coverage. A substantive search returning no relevant results describes that search only, not absence of demand or proof that a trend does not exist.

For current creator/trend claims, define a recent window appropriate to the question, inspect relevant dated originals across creators, compare with earlier/channel context and distinguish reposts from separate events. Capture visible metrics with observation dates and comparable observations before claiming growth. A transcript supports speech, frames support what was visually inspected, and captions/descriptions support their own text. A carousel caption does not substitute for its slides; a transcript does not establish a visual trend or video inspection. Follow the shared [creator evidence rules](../../../../docs/startup-skills/research-guide.md#creator-material-and-attention-signals).

Before a final comparison, reconcile **every required source obligation and supplied reference** against actual evidence or the user's explicit scope change. Fifty completed assessments cannot compensate for an unfulfilled required source family. Preserve accepted assessments, but mark the campaign **research incomplete** while its coverage remains incomplete. Never turn inaccessible evidence into a finding against an idea.

In the chat result, state material coverage limits **before recommendations**, not only inside the saved report. Briefly say which required platforms were explored and how (for example, publisher transcript versus video frames), which supplied links remain unread and how that limits the conclusion. For a paused run, give the precise missing input and resume task; provisional ideas remain provisional. A completed report may retain ordinary sampling limits, but cannot hide a missing required platform or imply that content was watched when only text was read.
