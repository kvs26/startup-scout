## Working preferences

- Wants everyday language that explains the customer's situation, what the product or provider does, what is delivered and how payment works.
- Prefers a natural, direct, specific explanatory voice. Bill Bryson's way of making hard subjects understandable is a writing reference; clear meaning matters more than polished sounding phrases.
- Wants concrete examples clearly distinguished from actual customer evidence.
- Wants summaries to explain domain knowledge needed and what to learn before implementation.
- Wants one Markdown file in `scouting-runs/` per idea-scout session/run, containing the comparison, full assessments and evidence. Continue an existing run in that file. This replaces per-idea and batch files without reducing research depth; the previously requested winning-opportunity folder remains separate.
- For open discovery, wants at least 50 distinct ideas researched deeply and compared before the scout recommends the strongest. This supersedes the earlier one-idea research approach. Save the strongest recommended idea in `opportunities/` and briefly mention 3–5 other top ideas in chat, keeping those in scouting records unless selected. If none is recommended, wants the top 3–5 explained anyway so they can choose what to discuss, even when they disagree with the scout. Substantial explanations must still cover the business, evidence, learning, customer access, time and budget.
- Wants commercially grounded discovery across industries, audiences and business models. Practical problems, convenience, entertainment, status, emotional experiences and other wants are eligible; novelty and an unsolved problem are not required. Assess competitive advantages such as price, value, experience and distribution.
- Wants substantially more diversity than repeated compliance/insurance ideas. Use the industry atlas as a search map. These sectors remain eligible when relevant, especially for a supplied idea.
- If no credible candidate emerges, wants a specific diagnosis and correction of the search method together with the top 3–5 researched discussion options. Believes large existing markets can accommodate new competitors and expects the scout to investigate how to sell within them, rather than require an untouched market. This is a search preference, not evidence that any particular entrant will succeed.
- For broad scouting with no supplied scope, wants an initial menu of randomly sampled industries plus saved ideas/problems from [user-leads.md](.agents/skills/startup-idea-scout/references/user-leads.md), then chooses a few directions before research starts. Within those choices, expects the scout to identify buyer groups and do substantial discovery and competitor checks itself. This supersedes the earlier preference to leave all initial area selection to the scout; the user is still not responsible for its desk research.
- Uses [user-leads.md](.agents/skills/startup-idea-scout/references/user-leads.md) as a notebook for ideas, personal problems and inspiration. Saving a lead is separate from choosing to research it.
- Declined the first six ideas because the summaries felt shallow and the ideas did not fit their profile. Requested deletion of those records. Fresh discovery should move to new proposals; revisit a declined proposal only at the user's request.
- Welcomes thorough MCQ interviews and also answers in freeform text. Questions should appear directly in chat: earlier tool-only questions were not visible to the user.
- Requests that startup skills automatically maintain this shared profile as relevant information changes.
- Wants each project workflow skill to know the related skills through the root [skill map](SKILL-MAP.md), updated as skills are added or changed. Wants a concrete next plan after scouting, including the appropriate next skill and handoff.
