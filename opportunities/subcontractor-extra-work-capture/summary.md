# Subcontractor extra-work capture

We would sell electrical and other specialist construction contractors software that helps record additional work at a building site and pass the details to their office.

When a crew does work beyond the original job, the office needs to know who requested it, what was done, which workers and materials were used, and whether charging for it was authorized. The proposed tool would turn photos and short descriptions into draft work records, ask for missing information and track office review.

## An example

**Imagined example:** An electrical crew installs an additional circuit after a request at the site.

1. The crew leader sends a photo and a description, including the job, requester, hours and materials.
2. The tool organizes that information into a draft record and prompts for anything missing.
3. The office checks the draft, sees whether permission to charge is recorded, and exports the reviewed details for its billing process.

Recording that work happened does not establish permission to charge for it. The tool would help track both facts; a person still has to resolve missing approval. This example describes the proposal, not a completed trial.

## Who would pay, and for what?

The contractor's owner or operations manager is a possible buyer. Crew leaders and office staff would use the product.

The proposed model is a monthly subscription per company, plus setup for its project rates and office formats. The price and payment terms are unset. A service that configures existing software is another possible way to meet the need ([possible buyer and model](research.md#c004)).

## What the research tells us

One contractor reports delayed records and unbilled extra work. However, that author later invites people to try their own product, so the account may also serve a promotional purpose. Other discussions describe tracking problems, while replies report satisfactory tools and better written-approval procedures ([problem accounts](research.md#c001)).

Clearstory already offers relevant work records and has documented voice-based draft creation in an open beta. SubTrack is another focused competitor. Photos, voice input and automated drafting alone do not establish a new offer customers need ([existing alternatives](research.md#c002)).

## What delivering it would take

We would need a capture method crews will actually use and records the office can review and export reliably. Project setup, rates, duplicate records, corrections and training could require continuing founder effort. The chosen messaging channel still needs testing.

Software usage, messaging, storage, onboarding and support costs are unpriced ([delivery and costs](research.md#c003)). An electrical-contractor association offers a possible discovery route, but the directory results were not accessible and no willing buyer is known ([customer access](research.md#c004)).
