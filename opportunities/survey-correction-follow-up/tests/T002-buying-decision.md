# T002 — Will an authorized buyer purchase a defined follow-up pilot?

Prepared: 2026-10-17  
Opportunity/current offer: [summary](../summary.md), assessment 2026-10-17  
Status: Proposed and conditional on a genuine gap in [T001](T001-workflow-and-alternatives.md), safe delivery review and user authorization for a live offer. No offer made or sale observed.

## What this should teach us

**Claim and decision:** [C004](../research.md#c004). A daily user wanting cleaner handoffs does not establish an organization can or will buy. A real purchasing decision on a fixed scope and actual terms would justify a limited delivery trial; inability to identify a budget owner or a clear refusal would challenge the business model.

**Relevant participants:** Project manager, survey-firm owner or institutional budget holder with actual authority or a known approval path, plus the coordinator who would use the product. Confirm who owns the study contract, software budget, data rights and vendor approval. The same person may hold several roles; job title alone does not prove authority.

## How to learn it

First reconstruct the last purchase of a fieldwork tool or outside data service: who proposed it, what line paid, approvals/security/contract steps, invoice terms, payment delay, and what would make a new vendor ineligible. Use the exact handoff gap found in T001 to prepare a one-page offer and a functioning synthetic-data demonstration. A candidate **test offer only**, to be revised for real delivery costs and actual customer needs, is ₹3,000 for one active study-month with one issue-file format, up to three staff roles and two hours of setup, no automatic renewal; any extra mapping/support needs a written price. The amount and limits are hypothetical, not a market price or a commitment to deliver. Before making a real offer, confirm the product can be delivered safely, obtain any required expert review and have the user authorize outreach/terms.

Ask the authorized buyer for the decision their normal process permits: a paid pilot order, signed purchase approval, or documented procurement step with named owner and date. Record an explicit decline and its reason with equal care. A vague “would pay” answer, expression of interest or request for a demo is not a purchase. Track the proposed price, total scope, payment terms, decision date, approval chain, objections, competing budgets and whether the buyer expects additional setup or support.

**Effort, cost and dependencies:** Prototype, practitioner and technical review, offer preparation, procurement time and possible security documents are unpriced. The candidate ₹3,000 fee may be insufficient once support is measured; [economics](../economics.md) shows only a partial scenario. No outreach, contract, invoice, purchase or customer pilot is authorized by this plan.

## How to interpret the result

| Possible observation | Effect on next decision | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An authorized buyer follows their real process and places a paid order or completes a binding approval for the defined pilot, with terms the founder can meet. | Run a limited delivery/use test and measure costs, support and later behavior in [T003](T003-continued-use.md). | Renewal, scalable acquisition or profit. |
| Buyer declines because current tools suffice, vendor/data requirements cannot be met, or the full cost exceeds value. | Revise the offer only if a concrete narrower segment/requirement emerges; otherwise leave it. | Whether every other organization would decline. |
| A user praises the idea or a manager says payment is possible but no real budget/approval step occurs. | Keep willingness to pay unresolved; learn the actual authority and terms if still material. | Purchase intent strong enough for a revenue claim. |
