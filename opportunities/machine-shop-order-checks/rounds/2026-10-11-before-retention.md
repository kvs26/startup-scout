# Machine-shop order checks

Folder identity: machine-shop-order-checks  
Current findings assessed: 2026-10-10  
Stage: Initially researched candidate; recent demand and residual gap unresolved

Small machining shops receive a customer purchase order, compare it with their quote and drawing, and release work to production. The proposed product checks part identifiers, revisions, quantities, units, prices and dates across those files, then presents differences and uncertain matches for a human to review. Initial market is open; available evidence mainly concerns English-speaking shops, including the US.

**Illustration, not an observed use:** A shop quoted revision C for 40 parts. An incoming order names revision B and 30 parts. The office uploads the documents and gets a source-linked discrepancy report before releasing the job. This illustration does not claim the product can discover undocumented physical changes.

**Proposed offer:** Focused upload-and-review tool, sold per shop with a usage allowance and optional setup. Price and terms unset.

## What we have learned

Older practitioner accounts support file/version friction, but current frequency and preventable cost are uncertain ([C001](../research.md#c001)). Paperless Parts already offers revision extraction and drawing comparisons, and OrderDiff offers generic order checks. Shared folders and careful review can also suffice ([C002](../research.md#c002)).

The possible reason to buy is a useful cross-document check with little setup. That distinction is unverified. Initial work includes parsing, matching, evidence display, review and file export; real samples and a machining/quality reviewer are dependencies. Supplier costs and founder support effort remain unpriced ([C003](../research.md#c003)). Association search found possible company routes, but direct directory reads failed; access to buyers is unknown ([C004](../research.md#c004)).

## What happens next

**Recommendation:** Tentative first investigation among this round's three candidates because the workflow can be examined with past files and need not initially replace business systems. Evidence freshness is a weakness. Confirm a recent, detectable mistake and assess incumbent coverage before building.

**User selection:** None recorded.

**Next unresolved question:** Would a real shop's present process miss a discrepancy our proposed report could expose, and would that shop pay to use it regularly?
