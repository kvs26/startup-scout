# Machine-shop order checks

We would sell small machining shops software that checks a customer's paperwork before the shop starts making parts. A machining shop makes parts from a customer's drawings. The office needs to confirm that the customer's order matches the shop's quote and the correct drawing version.

The proposed tool would compare those documents and highlight differences in part numbers, drawing versions, quantities, units, prices and dates. A person at the shop would decide whether each difference needs resolving.

## An example

**Imagined example:** A shop quoted 40 parts using drawing version C. The customer's purchase order, the document confirming what they want to buy, names 30 parts and version B.

1. An office worker uploads the quote, order and drawing.
2. The tool flags the different quantities and drawing versions, showing where each value appears in the files.
3. The worker checks the differences with the customer before releasing the job to production.

The software could find disagreements present in the supplied documents. If the customer changed a part without documenting that change anywhere, comparing files would not reveal it ([proposed scope](research.md#c003)). The example is illustrative; no product trial has been completed.

## Who would pay, and for what?

A shop owner, manager or operations lead is a possible buyer. The proposed model is a subscription per shop, including an allowance for order checks, with optional paid setup. The price and payment terms are unset.

A subscription would need enough useful repeat work to justify it. Occasional mistakes might instead suit occasional checks or a service ([possible buyer and model](research.md#c004)).

## What the research tells us

Older practitioner discussions describe difficulty managing drawing versions and inconsistent paperwork. We do not yet know how often the proposed customers face these problems now, or what losses this tool could prevent ([problem accounts](research.md#c001)).

Paperless Parts already offers related document and drawing checks. OrderDiff offers order comparisons, while shared folders and careful manual review work for some shops. A focused check across the customer's order, quote and drawing might be useful, but a reason to buy another product remains unproven ([existing alternatives](research.md#c002)).

## What delivering it would take

We would need real document samples and a reviewer who understands machining or quality control. The tool must read files reliably, match the correct parts and show uncertain results for review. Poor scans and unusual shop formats could create substantial setup and support work.

Software usage, storage, expert review and founder support costs are unknown ([delivery and costs](research.md#c003)). Trade associations suggest ways to find shops, but directory access was incomplete and no buyer access is established ([customer access](research.md#c004)).
