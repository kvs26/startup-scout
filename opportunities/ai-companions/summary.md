# AI Companions

We would sell adults an enjoyable ongoing conversation with an original fictional character: someone with a recognizable personality who can pick up yesterday's topic and remember details the user wants kept. The customer opens a mobile-friendly website, talks about their day or interests, and gets a personal, playful conversation. The initial audience I would pursue is English-speaking adults who already enjoy companion apps and are open to meeting another character.

**Imagined example:** A customer mentions an upcoming interview. The companion responds in its own warm, slightly witty voice and offers to remember the interview date. The customer accepts or corrects the memory. On a later visit, the character can ask how it went. If the customer deletes that detail, the product should stop retrieving it. The result being purchased is enjoyable continuity, rather than a completed work task.

The proposed first offer is **US$9/month for one companion and up to 1,000 text replies**, with a clear usage counter, editable memories and conversation export/deletion. A one-time 100-reply trial lets someone try the personality before opting into payment. These are investigator-selected test terms. The user selected the AI Companions business on 2026-10-18; they did not supply this price, audience or feature specification.

## The first product and repeat value

Start with one carefully directed character, a simple original visual identity, responsive text chat and a memory notebook the customer controls. During setup, the customer can give a preferred name, interests and conversation boundaries, then start chatting immediately. A saved preference should improve the next visit without repeatedly asking the user to configure a bot. After cancellation, retain a clearly disclosed export/deletion route; payment buys ongoing service, not ownership of a real relationship.

The reason to return is liking the character and enjoying how a familiar conversation develops. Friendly everyday companionship is the first working proposition; the selected category remains broader. I would keep this initial supplier/cost assessment adult and non-explicit. Voice, animation and additional characters could expand the experience after people demonstrate continued paid use. Eventually, original characters and creator collaborations could grow a catalog, provided creative quality, rights, acquisition and support scale with it. None of that expansion is validated.

AI can help produce the code, writing and visuals through the founder's separate production workflow. The founder still directs personality and style, judges conversations, handles users and decides whether a release is acceptable. No product or creative assets were produced in this investigation.

Our proposed reason to be chosen is **a particular character people like, with easy continuity and clear payment terms**. That is an untested experience advantage. Memory alone is not novel, and a lower monthly price does not make us cheaper than every annual or free alternative. We do not need to persuade everyone to abandon another app, but we must earn a share of their time and spending.

The strongest case against this version is that users can get comparable features, an established character and more freedom elsewhere. A small product only wins if its actual experience is preferable to someone. The earlier scout's negative verdict on a generic memory-based pitch remains part of the history; the user's selection authorizes investigating the opportunity, not rewriting that evidence.

## Building and operating costs

The essential work is character direction, reliable chat and memory, account isolation, deletion/export, usage limits, billing and support. Quality checks must cover ordinary conversations across days, wrong or corrected memories, interruptions and model upgrades. Do not use a polished demo as evidence that these work in continuing use.

At a current text-model price benchmark, an assumed 600 monthly replies with 6,000 input tokens and 250 billable output tokens each, plus 25% extra model work, cost **about $1.82/customer/month**. A $9 fee, hypothetical 5% payment/refund allowance and $0.75 other variable allowance leave **about $5.98 before acquisition, fixed expenses, taxes and your time**. At 1,000 replies with longer history and output, generation alone becomes $9.06. [Formulas and sensitivity](economics.md).

The model has not been tested for companion quality. Supplier terms also affect the product: the benchmark requires a suitable adult audience and paid-data handling; it does not justify promising zero data retention. Intimate conversation, difficult disclosures, age access and country-specific obligations need competent operational design. Applicable jurisdiction review and specialist costs remain unpriced. ## Fit with your profile

Your plan to direct AI production and personally handle customers is compatible with a small first sample. Your experience publishing basic websites supports that starting point. It does not yet establish reliable conversational delivery, private-data handling, customer acquisition or ongoing support.

With about ₹5,000/month and 10–19 hours/week, I would fund a small learning and evaluation exercise. A public multimedia companion service has not been shown to fit. The cost model's five-user example consumes about ₹4,284/month under its explicitly hypothetical currency/tooling allowances; ten users exceed the budget before acquisition, tax or specialist review. This is a promising category to test with discipline, not an established passive-income product.

## Domain knowledge needed

Learn what companion users enjoy, why they pay, and what makes a character feel inconsistent or intrusive. Learn the difference between saving a fact and reliably recalling the right fact, since a confidently invented memory damages the central experience. Understand subscription cancellation and usage limits from the customer's perspective. For release, you also need enough knowledge to assess data handling, adult access, difficult disclosures and safe behavior; AI-generated assurances are not independent verification.

Start with current-user conversations and a character brief. Then direct the separate production workflow to build a small sample and evaluate ten synthetic multi-day scenarios: correct/incorrect recall, a changed preference, deletion, a return after a gap, topic changes, contradictions, a distressing disclosure, excessive dependence and cancellation. The exercise should let you explain why a response is acceptable and how the product recovers from failure. These are proposed checks, not completed tests. Specialist review may be needed where your judgment is insufficient; nobody has been engaged or priced. Learning within several weeks is a planning aim, not a demonstrated readiness date.
