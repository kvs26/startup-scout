# T001 — What earns a place in an adult user's routine?

Prepared: 2026-10-18. Status: **proposed; no recruitment or conversations performed**.

Opportunity: [AI Companions](../summary.md). Claim/decision: [C001](../research.md#c001), [C002](../research.md#c002) and [C004](../research.md#c004). Learn what a customer actually values before directing the first product sample.

## Participants and scope

Recruit adults who used an AI companion recently, including paying users, satisfied free users and people who cancelled. The user and payer will often be the same person; confirm this instead of assuming it. English-speaking participants from verified reachable communities fit the current proposition. Record their country so later availability can be assessed.

Start with a workload batch of six conversations, aiming for a mix of these experiences. Six is a manageable learning allocation, not a representative sample or pass threshold. Recruitment access is unknown. Do not target people because they disclose a crisis or exploit intimate posts for personalized pitches.

## Procedure and material

Customer-connect prepares routes, checks current community rules, and drafts invitations for the founder. The founder conducts the conversations after arranging voluntary participation. A product is not required for this first test. Show a short, clearly labeled proposed-offer description only after discussing existing behavior.

1. Ask them to describe their most recent enjoyable conversation: when they opened the app, what they wanted, and what made the response work.
2. Ask what brings them back, what they have paid recently, what one payment bought, and who made the buying decision. A reported payment remains self-report unless independently supported; receipts or private logs are not required.
3. Ask about the last disappointing interaction, what they tried to fix it, and whether they cancelled, switched, or stayed. Ask satisfied users why their current app is sufficient.
4. Ask what would be lost by starting another companion: history, a preferred personality, customization, imagery, voice or community. Ask whether another product would be an addition or replacement.
5. Explain the proposed original character, editable recollections, 100-reply trial and $9/month for 1,000 replies. Ask what they expect to happen and which limits would interfere with their actual use. Do not interpret a compliment or hypothetical “yes” as payment.
6. Ask whether they want to hear about a future comparison when a real sample exists. This is voluntary future interest, not a purchase or a scheduled test.

Record participant pseudonym, adult eligibility, market, current product/tier, recent episode, reported payment, alternatives, switching effort, response to the allowance, and reasons for or against trying this specific experience. Keep observed events, direct quotes and researcher interpretation distinct. Offer synthetic examples instead of collecting intimate conversation histories. Record consent before storing anything identifiable.

## Interpretation

| Observation | Consequence | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Several independently recruited people describe a recurring need for this particular tone/experience and concrete limitations in their current setup | Use those details to direct one sample and invite a comparison | Accepted price, reliable delivery, population demand or renewal |
| Users already get the desired experience, or mainly require capabilities excluded from the first version | Revise the character/experience or its scope within AI Companions before building | That the entire companion category is unattractive |
| Praise without a recent use episode, unclear spending, or conflicting preferences | Keep the relevant claims unresolved and seek more specific evidence | A buying commitment |
| No suitable participants found | Change the recruitment route and record the access failure | Absent demand |

Allocate an assumed 20–30 minutes per conversation plus note synthesis; six conversations and analysis might take 4–6 founder hours, excluding recruitment. Compensation, if used, is unpriced and should not depend on favorable feedback. No incentives, invitations or contacts were sent.
