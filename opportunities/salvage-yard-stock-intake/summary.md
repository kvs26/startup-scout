# Faster stock listings for architectural salvage yards

We would sell software to independent US salvage yards that resell used doors, windows, fixtures and other building materials. Much of their stock consists of one-off items. Staff must photograph each item, measure it, describe damage, record where it is stored and keep its online availability current. The proposed tool would let staff upload phone photos and type a short measured note, then turn those facts into an item record in the yard's existing stock software and website. The yard owner or manager would approve the purchase; yard staff would operate it.

**Imagined example:** A worker photographs a used door and enters, “D47; width 32 inches, height 80 inches, thickness 1⅜ inches; damaged hinge area; rack B2; $120.” AI fills a draft with those labelled dimensions, condition and price, retaining the original photographs and keeping rack B2 as an internal staff field. The worker corrects it and clicks approve. The proposed connection saves the approved item in the yard's stock system and publishes its website listing; that successful save ends this cataloguing task. The intended saving is typing the same facts into several fields and separately preparing a description. Whether it is faster than existing tools is untested.

Customers could then search by size and ask staff to hold the door. The yard's existing sales system would remain the authority for holds, sales and returns; the connection would update the website from that system. If the connection fails, the proposed tool would flag the item for staff review rather than claim its availability is confirmed. Update timing and recovery rules still need agreement and testing. AI would leave unknown measurements blank. The yard must define required fields and resolve conflicting notes before publication.

The proposed first subscription would cover one yard, door-listing drafts and approvals, and a connection to one existing stock system. **US$59/month plus US$150 setup** is a price hypothesis. Setup would map one existing item file and configure that connection. Item and staff limits, support scope and any outside system fees remain to be priced; unlimited manual data cleanup is not included. The benefit to test is faster recording of newly received stock. It carries no promise of additional buyers. Expanding to more categories and stock systems could create a durable subscription business, but that growth is unproven.

There is direct evidence of the work: an Australian salvage-yard operator says cataloguing everything is too difficult. US operator Second Use keeps descriptions deliberately short to maintain intake speed and already automates inventory updates. It also explains why staff must control holds. These are operating accounts, not requests to buy our software. See [The Salvage Yard’s account](https://www.thesalvageyard.com.au/faq/) and [Second Use’s practices](https://seconduse.com/frequently-asked-questions/). [ThriftCart](https://thriftcart.com/pricing) already sells reuse-store software, Square provides a free base sales system, and [Treecat](https://wiki.treecat.io/ai-listing-from-photos) creates AI listing drafts. Our possible advantage is a quicker measured-note-to-listing workflow that fits the yard's existing system. That advantage has not been demonstrated.

The first customer route would be individual salvage yards and agencies maintaining their websites or sales systems. Initially agencies would refer yards; a reseller arrangement is not assumed. You would need access to a willing yard's intake staff and an authorized stock export or integration, then show that the workflow reduces their work. No customer, agency partner or sales channel is secured. At thirty subscriptions, gross revenue would be US$1,770/month, before tools, payments, sales, tax and your time. The model fails if you personally correct every item: even two minutes per item at 500 items would take nearly seventeen hours for one customer.

AI would help build the importer, listing interface and connection to the existing stock system, and draft item descriptions from the supplied facts. You would direct revisions and check performance against real examples. Yard staff must supply and approve physical measurements and condition; AI cannot certify an item is sound or suitable. You would remain responsible for directing and reviewing the software work, arranging hosting, monitoring failed updates, onboarding yards and providing support. Any specialist development help and its cost are unresolved. Neither faithful transfer of the supplied facts nor correct sale updates have been tested. The first stock-system connection is not yet chosen, and permission to use it is unconfirmed. Your current 10–19 hours/week could support a narrow investigation; delivery costs, integration rights and specialist help must be priced before assuming the ₹5,000/month starting budget is sufficient.

## Domain knowledge needed

You need to understand how salvage yards identify one-off stock, distinguish an item’s measurements from the space it must fit, describe damage, store stock and handle holds, sales and returns. These details determine which fields matter and prevent a sold or unsuitable item being presented incorrectly. Your knowledge is unassessed. Start by following thirty items as an experienced worker receives and records them, using the same authorized examples for the later comparison. You should then be able to explain which measurements and condition details are essential, who checks them, and which system decides whether an item remains available. Dependable delivery requires further software and operational validation.
