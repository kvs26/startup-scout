# Knitting pattern checks — research

Current claim assessment: 2026-10-11  
Scope: Technical editors/publishers checking written knitting patterns across sizes. Initial notation/language would be English; country and audience size unestablished.

## C001

**Claim:** Published knitting patterns can contain count, chart, instruction and size-consistency problems.

**Assessment and confidence:** Supported as reported incidents ([E001](#e001)), without verification of named patterns. An arithmetic defect differs from taste, individual gauge, skill level and fit preference. The same thread contains satisfied buyers and ways to avoid disappointing patterns.

**Buyer distinction:** Readers experiencing errors are not automatically software payers. Proposed buyers are editors/designers handling repeated releases; evidence for their recurring unmet need is weaker. Existing workflows are described in [E002](#e002) and [E006](#e006).

**Next useful check:** Examine an authorized draft and actual editor correction history: which defects cost time and which were caught before publication.

## C002

**Claim:** A specialist checking workbench could earn a place in an editor's workflow.

**Assessment and confidence:** Challenged for a generic checker. Stitchwright already follows readable instructions across sizes and offers editor collaboration ([E003](#e003), [E004](#e004)). Stitchmastery checks chart counts and generates instructions ([E005](#e005)). A generic pattern checker is not an established gap.

**Residual hypothesis, not an observed customer request:** Help an editor verify complex shaping, cables, separate garment sections and chart/text agreement in existing drafts, with a visible calculation trace and explicit unparsed sections. One competitor lists limitations for several such constructs; this documents scope, without proving demand or absence elsewhere. A spreadsheet and human review may remain preferable ([E002](#e002), [E006](#e006)).

**Next useful check:** Compare the normal method and accessible competitors on the same complex draft. Investigate only if a consequential repeated task remains and the editor values assistance.

## C003

**Claim:** Licensing to repeat professional users is plausible, but domain verification and low-price alternatives constrain the offer.

**Assessment and confidence:** Unresolved. An editor/studio could pay per seat/year or a designer per release; proposed models only. No price accepted. Stitchwright advertises low pilot prices and free invited-editor accounts ([E004](#e004)). Human editing ([E006](#e006)) is an alternative; its price is not a software budget.

**Initial delivery:** Import limited written notation, maintain size-specific stitch states, trace increases/decreases/repeats, link findings to source lines and let editors resolve ambiguity. If AI aids extraction, an accepted structured interpretation and deterministic calculation still need expert verification. Unparsed text cannot count as verified. Charts, short rows and branching pieces add substantial semantic complexity.

**Solo requirements/cost:** An experienced technical editor and authorized drafts with known corrections are dependencies. Parser development, expert validation, import, updates, support and acquisition are unpriced. Local calculation is a possible architecture, without proof of low total cost. No prototype or benchmark built.

**Next useful check:** Establish expert collaboration and useful coverage on actual drafts before estimating effort or pricing.

## C004

**Claim:** Editor/designer directories offer a narrow route to professional customers or partners.

**Assessment and confidence:** Relevant directory/service profiles are readable ([E006](#e006), [E007](#e007)); availability, interest and authority unknown. Hobby designers may have little reason to pay.

**Next useful check:** Identify an editor with the required specialties and repeat work; directory inclusion alone does not establish fit.

## E001

**What it reports:** Size-specific count disputes, chart/written disagreement during test knitting, readability problems and unsatisfactory construction/grading. Other replies describe good purchases, successful Ravelry project-note checks and issues unrelated to arithmetic.

**Context:** Purchasers/test knitters, mainly English discussion; defects and purchases unverified. Crochet reports excluded from the proposed knitting scope.

**Dates:** Events and exact publication date unknown (displayed seven months ago); retrieved 2026-10-11.

**Claims and limits:** C001–C002. One thread does not establish prevalence, editor time cost or designer willingness to buy software.
