# Family letter editions

We would offer families a paid service that turns scans of old handwritten letters into a checked, typed collection they can read and share. Software would produce a first draft. A person who can read the handwriting would check it against the original pages and prepare the finished files.

The customer is someone who owns a substantial collection of family letters and wants help finishing that work.

## An example

**Imagined example:** Meera has scanned 100 pages of her grandmother's letters. She wants relatives to read them without each person having to decipher the handwriting.

1. She sends us the scans. We inspect a few pages and agree what the finished collection should include.
2. Software drafts the typed text. A reviewer checks the words, names and dates, marking anything they cannot read confidently.
3. Meera receives the typed letters and original scans, with notes connecting passages to their original pages and a list of names and where they appear.

If she wants a shorter selection for a family book, she approves which letters or passages go into it. She also keeps the full typed collection. The example illustrates the offer; no such customer project has been completed.

## Who would pay, and for what?

The owner would pay a fee for completing an agreed project, quoted after we inspect a sample. That fee would need to cover software, checking, organizing the files and corrections. Additional volumes could be separate projects.

The price and payment schedule have not been decided. No customer has accepted this offer ([proposed delivery and fee](research.md#c003)).

## What the research tells us

People describe spending substantial time typing and checking family letters. One commenter has six scanned volumes and wants help choosing material for a finished collection. They say they can afford help, but no purchase is reported. The discussion's original author also promotes an app, which limits how much weight we should put on their speed claims ([problem accounts](research.md#c001)).

Existing software such as Transkribus and professional transcription and family-book services already cover much of this work. Some owners enjoy doing it themselves. A reason to choose our service remains unproven ([existing alternatives](research.md#c002)).

## What delivering it would take

We would need a reviewer who can read the collection's language and handwriting. Human checking and preparation may be the largest costs; a complete project has not been timed or priced. The first country and language to serve remain undecided ([delivery and costs](research.md#c003)).

Family-history communities and professional genealogists could help us find customers or referrals. No willing buyer or partner has been found ([customer access](research.md#c004)).

## The next useful check

The existing recommendation is to investigate this first among the three newer ideas if delivering a service interests you. Examine a small collection, measure the work and compare the owner's desired result with existing services.

The key question is: **would an owner choose us and pay enough to cover all the work?**
