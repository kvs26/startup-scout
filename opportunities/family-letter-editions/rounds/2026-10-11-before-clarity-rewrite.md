# Family letter editions

Folder identity: family-letter-editions  
Current findings assessed: 2026-10-11  
Stage: Initially researched candidate; project demand and economics unresolved

Owners of large inherited letter collections want to read, preserve or share them, sometimes as a book. This proposed service completes a checked digital edition from their scans: full transcript, source-page references, marked uncertainties, index and optional owner-approved selection. Existing software assists the service. Geography remains open; initial delivery needs a competent reviewer for the chosen language/script.

**Imagined illustration:** Someone has several volumes of a parent's letters and wants a readable account of the family's years abroad. They supply scans, approve a sample and editorial approach, then receive an indexed edition and its underlying archive.

**Proposed offer:** Project fee after sampling; optional additional volumes. Price/payment terms unset. Subscription demand not assumed.

## What we have learned

Family-project accounts support substantial preparation work. One commenter describes a selection/edition task and unspecified ability to spend, without payment evidence. The original author promotes their own app, so their speed claims are not an independent benchmark ([C001](../research.md#c001), [C002](../research.md#c002)).

Strong alternatives include Transkribus, satisfactory existing apps and professional transcribers/editors. Some owners enjoy doing the work. Managed transcription and family-book services already offer completion, custom formats and source-linked editions. Careful delivery is a service proposition, with no established missing feature.

Human checking and editorial judgment may dominate cost. Processing prices supply one input; actual labour and complete cost are unmeasured ([C003](../research.md#c003)). Communities and APG offer possible discovery/referral routes, without verified buyer access ([C004](../research.md#c004)).

## What happens next

**Recommendation:** First investigation among the new directions if a scoped service is acceptable. A small real collection could resolve important questions before investing in a platform. This depends on reviewer capability, an owner wanting completion help, a reason to choose a new provider and a viable fee.

**User selection:** None recorded for this idea.

**Next unresolved question:** Will an owner pay enough for a completed edition to cover all human work, despite existing software and professionals?
