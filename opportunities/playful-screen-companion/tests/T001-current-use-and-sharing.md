# T001 — What makes an existing screen pet worth keeping or sharing?

Prepared: 2026-11-01. [Current proposition](../summary.md). **Status: proposed learning plan; no recruitment, outreach, test or results.** Platform and our own character are undecided. This plan can inform those decisions without pretending to test an unbuilt product.

## Decision and participants

The [demand comparison](../research.md#c001--what-the-three-styles-can-reasonably-be-recommended-for) recommends a calm personal pet with invited funny reactions. Learn whether current users' actual behavior supports that direction, and which devices/contexts matter. Include people still using a pet, people who removed one, and people who made/imported a particular character. Seek people who can describe a recent concrete use; include actual payers when available, separating the payer from a gift recipient. No access to these people is established.

## Procedure and record

Start with the participant's existing experience before showing our idea. Ask them to walk through the last time they used a pet: device, product/character, how they found it, what they did, and why they kept it visible or turned it off. Ask what happened after novelty faded, whether rewards/collection mattered, and what they used instead after stopping. With voluntary permission, a person may show a non-sensitive screen or settings; do not request private messages or unrelated screen contents.

For sharing, ask for the last actual instance: what was sent/shown, who chose to try it and what happened afterward. Distinguish the sender enjoying a joke from the recipient wanting the companion. For personal creation, ask what character they wanted, how they obtained it and where creation/import failed. Ask about the last real payment and what it bought, with cancellation/nonpurchase reasons; a competitor price or hypothetical willingness is not a payment.

Record individual accounts with dates, first/secondhand status, duration as reported, uncertainty, and any voluntarily supplied artifact separately. Do not turn several events from one person into independent customers. Avoid presenting three attractive descriptions and counting votes as market shares.

## Interpretation

| Observation | Decision affected | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People voluntarily keep a particular pet without collecting rewards and describe repeated enjoyment. | Strengthens personal companionship as a motive worth testing in our version. | Does not establish our unknown character's retention. |
| People mainly farm items, follow a licensed character, or quickly remove pets. | Reconsider audience, character strategy and expectations for a generic companion. | Does not rule out everyone else. |
| Senders share, but recipients rarely try or dislike it. | Prioritize voluntary recipient trials; weaken the assumed sharing route. | An interview cannot measure a referral rate. |
| Personalization matters but existing imports already satisfy it. | We need an experience people prefer, not just another editor. | Does not price that advantage. |
| Only hypothetical preferences, vague memories or conflicting accounts emerge. | Keep the claim unresolved; improve relevance and concrete questioning. | Do not manufacture a pass rate. |

This requires participant access and founder time; incentives, recruitment effort and sample size are unpriced. Startup-customer-connect would prepare routes and invitations if this plan is selected, without sending anything absent authorization. Return notes by referencing `playful-screen-companion/T001`. A later preference/recipient-use test would require an actual sample from the separate production workflow; neither this interview nor a proposed sample establishes our product's conversion, retention or revenue.
