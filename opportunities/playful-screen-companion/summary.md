# A playful pet on your screen

We are investigating a small screen pet people can make personal and send to a friend. My leading idea to test is to make the first visit feel like receiving a funny personal gift. The recipient can play immediately in a webpage, then optionally get the companion for everyday use. The user selected screen pets on November 1 and asked why someone would choose ours over free alternatives, with platform and AI feasibility also researched. They have not selected this gifting version or a platform.

**Imagined example:** Sam picks an original snack-loving dragon, names it Chip and writes “Guard your fries, Anya. From Sam.” Sam previews its reaction, copies the gift link and sends it to Anya through their usual messaging app. Anya opens it on a phone or computer. The page shows Chip and Sam's message. Tap Chip and it hides a packet of fries behind its back; left alone, it sleeps. Hide removes it from the page. Anya can tap Make one back to open a new copy, change its name/message and send her own link to Sam. Sam's original gift stays unchanged. These characters and the gift flow are unbuilt.

Choosing from a few already-made characters and reactions keeps this simpler than generating a new creature for every person. The free web pet stays inside its own page. Keeping it visible beside other activity would require an optional extension or app. Traffic and enjoyable use come first in this phase. Paid extra characters or custom-pet creation could follow; no payer offer, charging unit or price is selected.

## What the demand evidence supports

Existing pets show substantial adoption and individual accounts of attachment, gifts and customization spending. The [first comparison](research.md#c001--what-the-three-styles-can-reasonably-be-recommended-for) supports a personal pet with calm presence and deliberate funny reactions, without identifying a statistical style winner.

Further research finds that free alternatives already offer personal uploads, friend nudges, comic page reactions and web trials. The proposed reason to choose us is the whole gift experience: a friend made this for me, its reaction fits our joke, and I can immediately play and make one back. An ordinary GIF or free pet may be enough. No customer has demonstrated choosing our version. A one-time smile and wanting a lasting companion are separate outcomes. Own-pet/photo customization remains a deeper but harder alternative. [Four entry points and strongest contrary evidence](research.md#c003--a-reason-to-choose-us-must-survive-the-free-alternatives).

## Reaching people and producing the experience

The proposed route is an appealing original demonstration leading to a free, touch-friendly web try, then personal sharing and optional installation. The founder still needs to make/publish those demonstrations and find people who respond; no audience, referral rate or traffic forecast is established. Existing operators' discovery channels cannot be assumed for us.

A Chrome extension would keep the pet on supported computer webpages; a separate Android app would be needed beside native Instagram or games, with explicit overlay permission. Android publishing adds registration and, for new personal accounts, a qualifying closed test. Native desktop distribution is another scope. Web-first trial material is my recommendation; the lasting platform remains undecided. No iOS overlay capability is established. [Platform facts and tradeoffs](research.md#c004--platform-recommendation-separates-trying-from-keeping).

AI would help create a small set of original appearances/reactions and software. The founder directs retries and checks consistent faces/bodies, clean movement, comic timing and touch/hide behavior. Moving approved artwork with code is another route; it may be less expressive. Reusable assets avoid fresh model calls for each play. A newly generated animated pet for every visitor adds creation, retry and support costs. Supplier tools offer a possible bounded asset trial, but advertised capabilities do not prove our quality or build time. Learning character consistency, animation timing and platform behavior is necessary. No asset sample or fit within **₹5,000/month and 10–19 hours/week** has been demonstrated; hosting, tools, distribution and founder time remain separate costs. [Production and budget assessment](research.md#c005--ai-and-budget-reusable-assets-before-unlimited-custom-generation).

## Current decision and next step

**Selected by the user:** focus A, keep B aside, compare all pet styles, then research why-choose-us first while also covering platform and AI feasibility. The gift direction, dragon, supplier and platform remain investigator proposals.

**Recommendation:** start by testing one memorable original pet with reusable reactions and personal gifting, comparing it against an ordinary GIF/free pet. The user subsequently asks “what do you think is better”; this remains the investigator's judgment, rather than an explicit user selection of gifting. A friend supplies a reason to open the link, while approved assets can serve many people. Keep actual-pet/original-character recreation as a possible later paid expansion, conditional on desired likeness, quality and cost. The decisive unknowns are an enjoyable actual character and whether sender/recipient behavior supports lasting use beyond the first gift. Pricing, complete launch economics and earnings remain unresolved.
