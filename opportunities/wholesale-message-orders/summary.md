# Wholesale message orders

We would sell wholesalers software that turns customer messages into draft orders for their staff to check. A wholesaler supplies goods to other businesses, such as shops or restaurants.

Customers often describe products informally or refer to earlier orders. Staff must identify the customer, match the message to the wholesaler's product list, confirm quantities and pack sizes, then enter the order into their business system. The proposed tool would help with that entry work while preserving the customer's usual way of sending an order.

## An example

**Imagined example:** A shop messages its drinks supplier: “Same as Tuesday, but two fewer cases of cola.”

1. A staff member forwards or pastes the message, identifies the customer and supplies the relevant earlier order.
2. The tool drafts the order using the wholesaler's product list and adjusts the cola quantity.
3. Staff check the products, quantities and prices. If the message does not identify the right pack size, they resolve it before exporting a file for the existing order system.

The result is an order ready for staff review. Automatic stock allocation or customer confirmation would add work beyond the initial proposal ([proposed scope](research.md#c003)). No working integration or customer trial has been completed.

## Who would pay, and for what?

The wholesaler's owner or operations manager is a possible buyer. Order-entry staff would use the tool.

The proposed model is a monthly fee per branch, plus paid setup of the product list and the file format needed by its order system. The price and payment terms are unset ([possible buyer and model](research.md#c004)).

## What the research tells us

One practitioner discussion describes writing down message orders, entering them later and searching through chats. The author's identity, country and product category are uncertain. Many other search results were developers pitching tools or reposting the same account, which adds little independent customer evidence ([problem accounts](research.md#c001)).

Orderlion and Choco already advertise converting messages into orders while keeping the customer's ordering channel. A particular small wholesaler, language or system format might be poorly served, but that has not been demonstrated ([existing alternatives](research.md#c002)).

## What delivering it would take

We would have to handle informal product names, pack sizes, customer-specific prices, repeat orders and later changes. Setting up each wholesaler's product list and resolving everyday exceptions could create substantial support work.

Software usage, messaging access, imports, onboarding and support costs are unpriced. No WhatsApp integration or supplier entitlement has been checked ([delivery and costs](research.md#c003)). A distributor association lists real companies, but many may be larger than the intended customers; buyer access remains unknown ([customer access](research.md#c004)).
