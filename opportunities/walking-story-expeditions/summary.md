# Walking-story expeditions

An adult who likes cozy or fantasy games takes the same evening walk several times a week. We would sell a small original adventure that advances with those walks: places to discover, a character to accompany and a story with a satisfying ending. The customer operates a mobile app; the founder publishes and maintains the illustrated journeys. The initial market is English-reading adults internationally, with research concentrated in US and Australian app-store contexts.

Imagine choosing an island expedition. After an ordinary walk, you open the app and find that your traveler has reached a lighthouse. A short scene introduces its keeper and reveals the next destination. A later walk takes you toward a garden, then a harbor. You choose a manageable pace and can revisit completed scenes. The app needs to be enjoyable when checked afterward; it should not require watching a screen throughout the walk.

A **hypothetical $8 purchase** would buy one complete expedition and continued access to its scenes. An appealing second world could earn another purchase. This price and repeat-purchase behavior are untested for our product. The proposed business is an original game/content catalog, with software delivering it. It is not a forecast based on the Instagram post's revenue screenshots.

## The customer and what they want

Start with adults who already enjoy walking or walking games and want discovery, atmosphere and visible progress. They need not dislike their current app: a new world can be an additional entertainment purchase. A first expedition could last roughly four to eight weeks depending on the chosen pace, but pacing and content volume need observation before a promise.

Public evidence includes a reported path from a TikTok review to buying Fantasy Hike and interest in another themed journey. Other accounts describe long paid engagement with illustrated walking games. These are selected self-reports, not verified receipts or retention statistics. They support the category more strongly than demand for our particular island. Canonical observations remain in [I033 E001](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-18-consumer-apps.md#i033-e001) and [E003](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-18-consumer-apps.md#i033-e003).

## What the first version would do

The first commercial offer would contain one original world, a limited set of illustrated landmarks and short authored scenes, saved progress and one reliable phone step-data integration. A simple pace choice determines the distance between discoveries. Users can see what the purchase includes, recover progress and retain their completed story. No runtime AI is necessary. An authored sequence makes content costs finite and avoids promising endless personalized narrative.

The founder would create or commission the story and artwork, check consistency, implement the progress rules, test data import and handle support. The customer grants the relevant device permission and chooses their pace. Multiplayer, continuous GPS, public rankings, licensed franchises and an open-ended roleplaying system would add different costs and are outside this first offer.

A manually advanced six-landmark sample can answer questions about taste and pacing. It cannot establish the convenience, battery behavior or reliability of the finished mobile product. Those need a separate technical check and real-device use.

## Why someone might buy it again

The proposed reason is wanting to reach the next place in a world the buyer likes. New characters and journeys could create repeat sales without a mandatory subscription. A recognizable catalog could eventually support writer/artist collaborations and licensed creator worlds, but rights, production economics and repeat purchases must work before that expansion.

The largest commercial uncertainty is whether an unfamiliar original setting motivates people enough to pay. Existing demand may partly reflect attachment to familiar fantasy references. A generic map with generated paragraphs would not establish the necessary appeal. Neither liking a sample nor finishing a free journey proves willingness to buy the next one.

## Evidence and existing alternatives

The strongest support is the combination of reported purchases, sustained voluntary use, a visible discovery path and requests for further stories. The strongest contrary finding is the quality and breadth of existing alternatives. Fantasy Hike already has rich presentation and social/health integrations. Walkr offers a substantial free-entry illustrated game. The Walk already supplies an authored walking narrative with free introductory episodes, and WalkScape documents a walking RPG with a free beta-application route and paid supporter access. See the [canonical comparison and pricing limits](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-18-consumer-apps.md#i033-demand-and-current-alternatives).

Story unlocking, gentle checking and avoiding a constant on-screen map are therefore established category features. Our hypothesis is preference for a specific original world, a manageable journey and an understandable finite purchase. This is a creative-quality claim to test, not a missing-feature claim. A normal step counter, an audiobook or doing the walk without software remain easy alternatives. Existing collections and progress also make switching costly; selling an additional expedition is more plausible than demanding migration.

## How to find the first customers

The proposed route is a short demonstration linking an ordinary walk to an appealing landmark reveal, followed by a free opening and a visible full-journey price. The observed video-to-purchase account makes this more concrete than simply assuming people use social media. It still supplies no audience, conversion rate or affordable acquisition cost for the founder.

Cozy-game and walking creators are possible collaborators. The founder has no established relationship with them and their fees and interest are unknown. Original public samples can also attract an opt-in audience over time, consuming founder hours. No creator was contacted, no audience list obtained and no advertisement run during scouting.

## What building and running it requires

Initial work includes the content sequence, coherent art, data permissions, step-source selection and deduplication, saved-state recovery, accessible presentation, purchases/restoration and clear progress after app upgrades. Start with one mobile platform. Changes of phone, late-arriving step data, disabled permissions and timezone boundaries need testing. Repeated visits should feel rewarding without punishing missed days.

The scouting estimate of **70–120 hours for a convincing native slice** is a planning assumption, not a development quote. At 10–19 hours/week that is roughly four to twelve weeks before additional marketing or revisions. Content production can make it longer. A small manual/web sample should precede that commitment.

## Fit with your profile

The [founder profile](../../FOUNDER-PROFILE.md) supports software curiosity, reading interest, a willingness to learn for weeks and experience directing simple website work. Those help with a sample and product iteration. They do not establish game-writing, illustration, native integration, scaling or consumer distribution ability. Limited independent debugging makes step-data and account-recovery reliability a real gap.

This opportunity can begin with a bounded personal project and written creator outreach later, which fits the available hours better than a large live service. It still requires learning how to make entertainment people choose. Its overall fit is conditional, not demonstrated. Table-tennis challenges have stronger existing domain fit; walking stories lead this campaign because the paid-use and content-repeat case is better supported publicly.

## Domain knowledge needed

Learn how walking games turn ordinary movement into a satisfying sequence: distance between rewards, different walking speeds, useful stopping points, clarity when data arrives late, and the difference between an attractive screenshot and an enjoyable week. Understand original-content rights, consistent visual design, device step sources and permission expectations. These are product and game-design requirements; the offer makes no clinical outcome promise.

First, map one existing journey's progression and available alternatives. Then storyboard six original landmarks and manually advance them at slow, moderate and fast paces. The exercise should make it possible to explain why each discovery is worth reaching, where boring gaps occur and how missed days are handled. Separately, inspect a small step-data integration on a real device and explain how it avoids double counting. No such exercise has yet been completed. Public research cannot establish mastery within a fixed number of weeks, and professional art review may remain necessary.

## What happens next

**Recommendation:** investigate further through a small original experience. The paid entertainment mechanism is credible enough to pursue; preference for our world, affordable production and acquisition remain unproved. Do not commission a full catalog or promise a subscription before those questions improve.
