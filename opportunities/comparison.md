# Comparing the six business ideas

These are six proposed businesses with initial research. Each explanation below shows what a customer would buy and how it would work.

Wording revised: 2026-10-11. The family-letter, knitting and game findings were assessed on October 11; the machining, construction and wholesale findings remain from October 10. This rewrite uses that saved research. No customer has bought any of these proposed offers, and no idea has been selected.

## What each business would do

### 1. Family letter editions: prepare old letters for relatives to read

We would deliver a paid service. A family sends scans of handwritten letters; software drafts typed text, and a person checks it against the originals. We return the typed collection, original scans, notes connecting passages to their source pages, and a list of names and where they appear.

**Imagined example:** Meera has 100 pages of her grandmother's letters. She wants relatives to read them without deciphering the handwriting. We check and organize the collection for her. If she wants a shorter selection for a family book, she approves what goes into it.

The owner would pay a project fee after we inspect a sample. The price is unset. [Full summary](family-letter-editions/summary.md).

### 2. Knitting pattern checks: help editors find mistakes before publication

We would sell software to professionals who check knitting instructions. It would follow the number of stitches through each garment size, highlight mismatches and show the calculations. The editor would resolve unclear instructions and approve corrections.

**Imagined example:** A sweater pattern starts one size with 120 stitches, adds two four times, then says there should be 126. The tool would flag the expected 128 and point to the relevant lines. This simple example shows the mechanics; the business question concerns complicated, repeated work that existing tools leave to editors.

An editor or editing business could pay for a license, or a designer could pay per release. The model and price are unset. [Full summary](knitting-pattern-checks/summary.md).

### 3. Game localization release checks: find display problems in translated screens

We would sell small game studios a tool that captures chosen screens in different languages and screen sizes, then compares them with approved earlier screenshots. Reviewers would decide which changes are defects.

**Imagined example:** After a menu update, a translated button label is cut off on a small phone screen. The reviewer sees the old and new screens together and passes the finding to a developer. People still need to judge translation quality.

A studio could pay for a license with setup and support, or for a service checking an agreed release. The offer and price are unset. [Full summary](game-localization-release-checks/summary.md).

### 4. Machine-shop order checks: compare paperwork before parts are made

We would sell small machining shops software that compares a customer's order, the shop's quote and the drawing. It would highlight differences in part numbers, drawing versions, quantities, units, prices and dates for a person to review.

**Imagined example:** A shop quoted 40 parts using drawing version C, but the customer's order names 30 parts and version B. An office worker uploads the files, sees the differences and checks them with the customer before production.

The proposed charge is a subscription per shop with an order allowance and optional setup. The price is unset. A comparison can only reveal information present in the files. [Full summary](machine-shop-order-checks/summary.md).

### 5. Subcontractor extra-work capture: get site records to the billing office

We would sell specialist construction contractors software that turns site photos and descriptions into draft records of additional work. It would ask for missing details and track office review and export.

**Imagined example:** An electrical crew installs an extra circuit. Its leader records the job, requester, hours and materials. The office checks the draft and sees whether permission to charge is recorded before preparing billing paperwork. Proof that work happened and permission to charge are separate facts.

The contractor could pay a monthly company subscription plus setup for its rates and office formats. The price is unset. [Full summary](subcontractor-extra-work-capture/summary.md).

### 6. Wholesale message orders: turn informal messages into checked order drafts

We would sell wholesalers software that matches customer messages to their product lists and drafts orders for staff review. Staff would resolve unclear products, pack sizes and prices before exporting the order to their existing system.

**Imagined example:** A shop tells its drinks supplier, “Same as Tuesday, but two fewer cases of cola.” Staff identify the customer and earlier order, then review the proposed quantities and products.

The wholesaler could pay monthly per branch, plus setup of its product list and import format. The price is unset. Small food or drinks wholesalers are a proposed starting segment; the country and language remain undecided. [Full summary](wholesale-message-orders/summary.md).

All six examples are imagined. They explain proposed workflows, rather than completed trials, measured savings or buying decisions.

## Which deserves more investigation?

The existing recommendations belong to two separate research groups. This writing revision does not establish a new ranking from first to sixth.

**Within the three newer ideas, family letters comes first if delivering a service interests you.** A small collection could help us learn what the owner wants and how much work it takes, using existing software. It still needs a capable reviewer, a reason to choose us and a fee covering the work.

**Knitting comes second, provided we can work with an experienced editor.** The next step is to compare normal methods and existing tools on a complicated draft. If they already handle the important work well, the case for another checker weakens.

**The general game tool comes third and should wait.** Close alternatives already exist. It becomes worth reconsidering with a reachable studio and a consequential problem those options leave unresolved.

**Within the three earlier ideas, the October 10 order was machining, construction, then wholesale.** Machining could be examined using past paperwork; construction could move first with direct contractor access; wholesale needed a specific buyer with an existing-tool mismatch and a workable import format. Those recommendations have not been reassessed against the newer group.

On October 11, you said you did not like those three earlier ideas at present and asked to retain them for later reading. No reason was supplied. They remain here for that purpose, and no idea has been selected. A preference is separate from evidence about whether customers would buy.

## What remains unknown?

For every idea, we still need evidence from the relevant customer, a concrete reason to choose the offer over existing options, complete delivery costs and an actual buying decision. No interviews, private-project trials, accepted offers or repeat-use evidence are recorded.

The newer research mainly used English public communities, with one French historical-handwriting discussion. The older research also has market and source limits. These findings do not establish demand in India, broad international demand or the number of reachable buyers. Your specialist skills, customer access, budget, available time and income target remain unknown.

The original research used available web search when Tavily was unavailable. Some pages and directories could only be read partially. Education, coaching and several other directions were explored at different depths; the saved runs explain those leads and omitted markets. They were not investigated as part of this rewrite.

The original rounds stopped after checks found existing alternatives and left important questions dependent on real projects and customer decisions. Clearer explanations make those decisions easier to consider; they do not resolve the business uncertainties.
