## Previous summary

# Blind presentation party

A friend hosting a game night wants an activity that makes everyone laugh without asking guests to prepare anything. We would sell a downloadable party pack: players take turns presenting ridiculous slides they have never seen, pretending to understand what comes next. The host operates the files on a laptop or television. We create and sell the same finished pack repeatedly.

**Imagined example:** a player starts explaining “Why your fridge is running a secret nightclub.” The next slide reveals an absurd diagram, and they have to work it into their explanation. After three minutes, another friend takes over with a different unseen deck. This is an illustrative premise, not a completed or tested product.

The proposed **US$7 one-time purchase** covers twelve original short decks, a reusable offline browser player, a standard slide/PDF fallback, instructions and voting cards. The payer is the adult organizing the gathering; guests play free. The price and formats require testing. Start with English-speaking game-night and friends’ weekend hosts, using North American buyers as the first research and pricing reference. India remains eligible; a localized edition and rupee price need their own evidence.

This is a small owned consumer entertainment product. The scout recommends investigating it first because **earliest credible earnings** is the chosen priority: its estimated preparation and delivery are more bounded than personalized image products or installed games. Later editions could produce additional purchases because familiar decks lose their surprise. That growth requires continued writing and customer acquisition, rather than an assumed subscription.

## The customer and what they want

The trigger is an upcoming casual gathering. The organizer currently uses an existing party game, finds free decks, makes slides themselves or chooses another activity. A reported Talking Points player enjoyed a friend’s copy and eventually bought a discounted Jackbox bundle for their own party. That is actual attributed purchase behavior, although not independently verified. A separate 2026 friend-trip organizer wanted this exact kind of no-preparation activity but rejected paid and AI material. ## What the first version would do

The host tries one complete sample deck, sees the full-pack price, pays and downloads the files. At the gathering, the host opens the player, chooses an unused deck and advances slides while a friend improvises. A familiar slide/PDF format provides a fallback if the browser player fails. It does not require accounts for guests, live multiplayer infrastructure or custom decks for every buyer.

The founder uses AI assistance for drafts, layout and software, then edits the premises and tests the actual performance. The desired quality is a sequence that gives an ordinary willing player material to improvise with. A polished export or a skilled actor’s funny demo alone does not establish that quality. Product creation, playtesting and promotional footage have not happened in this scouting run.

## Why someone might buy it and keep using it

One payment buys an immediately usable evening activity with a known style, demonstrated by a full free sample. The proposed advantage is twelve well-edited, original decks aimed at friends, at a low one-time price. Offline delivery and one-time payment already exist elsewhere; neither is a unique invention.

A group may replay the game with unused decks, and a host may buy a later edition for new surprises. No repeat-purchase or renewal rate is known. The same creative work can be sold to many hosts, but continual fresh editions still take founder time.

[Current Jackbox offer](../../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e003).

The biggest doubt is paid preference for our humor. Free decks, DIY and a sale-priced Jackbox bundle can all win. A 2026 organizer’s explicit rejection of AI material also challenges any plan to sell unedited generated filler. The recommendation depends on original editing and real group enjoyment; it does not imply that the genre is a newly accelerating trend or that our version has been validated.

X can carry the same short performance and sample link. Show the $7 price and offer one complete free deck, then sell the full edition to hosts preparing a gathering.

The initial proposal is six clips from three decks over roughly ten days, with about eight founder hours for production and replies. No audience, willing participants, filming access or conversion rate is assumed. Organic posting does not ensure geographic targeting or free customer acquisition. Measure visits, sample use, paid orders and refunds separately; views or guest participation alone are not payment.

## What building and running it would require

The scoped estimate is **15–25 hours for the pack and delivery flow, plus 6–10 hours for playtesting, editing and initial demonstration material: 21–35 hours total**. At 10–19 hours/week, allow roughly two to four calendar weeks for a sellable test, conditional on available participants and competent output. This estimates preparation, not time to the first sale. Substantial content rewrites can extend it.

Required work includes original slide sequences, readable television layouts, fallback exports, payment/download recovery and ordinary-laptop checks. Support is mainly lost downloads, unzip/display questions and refunds. A fixed pack avoids per-customer generation and personalized revisions. Later marketing effort continues beyond the initial estimate; eight posting hours and two sales would mean four marketing hours per order before any support.

## Domain knowledge needed

Learn the difference between prepared presentation parties and improvised unseen-slide play; then learn pacing, visual surprises and giving players enough context without writing their entire performance. The [KAPOPO gameplay explanation](https://kapopo.com/faq/) is a verified introductory reference, not validation of our product.

The practical learning sequence is to observe or participate in a full game, explain its rules unaided, then assess a separately produced sample with ordinary players. Check whether players understand each slide from across a room, recover when the story changes and finish near the stated time. Record confusion and revisions as well as laughter. This exercise is proposed, not performed. No specialist qualification is required, and no paid comedy professional is assumed; if repeated editing cannot produce enjoyable play, the advantage fails.
