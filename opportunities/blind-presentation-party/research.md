# Blind presentation party — research

Evidence assessed: 2026-10-29; explanation and production qualification corrected 2026-10-30, without new external research. Scope: a proposed $7 downloadable game for English-speaking adult hosts. Guests give made-up presentations using slides they see for the first time while speaking. The proposed purchase covers twelve presentations, instructions and files the host can show without an internet connection. Prices, production quality and demand for this version remain untested.

Original observations retain their single home in the [scouting run](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003). This handoff links them rather than creating duplicate evidence entries. The coordinator and a different category researcher reviewed the finalist; no customer experiment occurred.

## C001

**Claim:** Some ordinary consumers enjoy improvised presentation games enough to spend money for a gathering.

**Assessment:** Moderate confidence in the occasion and some spending. The original Talking Points player reported buying a discounted bundle for a party after initially resisting its price. This is a single attributed account, not a transaction audit or evidence of the proposed $7 pack’s conversion. [I003 E001](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e001).

**Contrary evidence:** A 2026 friends’ weekend organizer specifically rejected paid and AI content. It establishes a real opposing preference, not the proportion of the audience who shares it. [I003 E002](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e002).

**Next useful check:** Upcoming hosts compare the actual priced sample with free alternatives. No minimum number of public anecdotes can settle that preference.

## C002

**Claim:** A low-priced, immediately usable pack with a distinct comic style could win an additional entertainment purchase.

**Assessment:** Plausible, low-to-moderate confidence; original humor is the unproved advantage. Jackbox is richer, may be discounted, and free decks can satisfy the same evening. One-time downloads already exist. [Jackbox offer](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e003), [public decks](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e005).

**Independent challenge:** Current PowerpointKaraoke.com packages start at $19.99/four decks, but inspected testimonials are mostly professional/educational. KAPOPO’s premium download constraints make instant access a relative convenience; free Plus AI decks remain a direct substitute. None proves our humor is better. [Challenge and source entries](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#challenge-trends-i003).

**Next useful check:** Ordinary willing players complete a sample without coaching; hosts prefer its specific content at the displayed price. If the free choice wins, the proposed advantage fails.

## C003

**Claim:** The founder can prepare and deliver this more predictably than personalized image products or installed games within the current limits.

**Assessment, qualified 2026-10-30:** Reusing the same files for many buyers plausibly reduces per-order work. That does not establish that the founder can produce entertaining presentations faster or more predictably than the alternatives. The hardest production step—AI-created content that ordinary players can use—was not demonstrated. The earlier 21–35-hour estimate is a planning assumption, not a supported preparation schedule or sufficient reason to rank this first for quick earnings. The [original delivery assessment](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003) and [comparison](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#final-comparison) remain dated history; no full-pool reassessment occurred here.

**Proposed production workflow, untested:**

| Sellable part | Intended AI work and founder direction | Quality check and remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topics and slide sequence | AI proposes premises and writes a sequence; founder chooses a direction and asks AI to revise confusing or repetitive parts. | An ordinary player can understand each slide, invent a response and continue the story. No sample or play session exists; effectiveness of AI revisions is unknown. |
| Pictures and readable layout | AI generates the intended pictures and arranges slides; founder checks whether images match the prompt and are readable across a room, then requests changes. | Consistency, rights/terms, readable text and whether the images support the activity need checking with actual outputs. Tools, access and costs are unselected. |
| Showing and delivering the files | AI creates the browser/slide files and payment-download flow; founder directs corrections after ordinary-laptop and television checks. | Display, download recovery and backup-file behavior have not been tested. Software generation does not establish enjoyable content. |

**Founder work and fit:** The founder intends to direct AI creation, decide what to keep and handle people. They must learn enough about the activity to notice where players get stuck and give useful revision requests. Personally rewriting the jokes or drawing replacement images is not an established fallback; if necessary, it would expose a conflict with the intended workflow. Willing-player access, judgment, tools, revision count and support effort remain unknown.

**Earlier effort assumptions retained:** 15–25 hours for the collection and delivery flow, plus 6–10 hours for initial playtesting/editing/demo material, totaling 21–35 hours. At the recorded weekly availability the earlier summary suggested roughly two to four weeks, conditional on participants and competent output. Recurring promotion was additional. These estimates did not establish the AI creation/revision process and must be reassessed before being used as a comparative advantage. The [previous summary](rounds/2026-10-30-explanation-correction.md#previous-summary) retains the original wording.

**Next useful check:** The investigator should specify a small representative presentation and what a separate production/play check must establish: whether AI can create and revise it through founder direction, whether ordinary players can complete it, what failed and how much time/credit the revisions used. Identify participants and tool costs. This correction defines the missing check; it does not create content or perform a test.

## C004

**Claim:** Short performance clips could attract paying hosts through Instagram or X.

**Assessment:** Low confidence. The observable gameplay provides a concrete demo, but no research establishes no-audience conversion for this founder. Free entertainment in the clip may be all viewers want. The proposed six-clip/eight-hour campaign is an assumption, not executed marketing.

**Contrary context:** A different novelty-product operator reported large sharing numbers but only $47 revenue, while a successful creator described substantial pre-existing audience work. These are illustrative, unaudited accounts, not conversion forecasts for presentation games. [Sharing/revenue example](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#shared-e008), [creator preparation](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#shared-e009).

**Next useful check:** Measure sample use and payments separately from views, and count filming/editing/reply hours. Identify a reachable adult host audience without assuming influencers or paid ads.

## C005

**Claim:** Small sales can have positive contribution, although startup recovery and cash receipt may be slow.

**Assessment:** Moderate confidence in fee arithmetic; low confidence in actual conversion/refunds/support. The earlier summary's detailed scenarios are retained here so the current summary can explain their practical implication. They are hypothetical calculations using dated inputs, not measured bills or accepted prices. [Fees](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#shared-e001), [hosting](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#shared-e006).

| Item per proposed $7 sale | Earlier input and status |
| --- | --- |
| Non-US card checkout | 6.5% × $7 + $0.50 = $0.955; sourced fee scenario, excluding customer tax effects |
| Download/delivery reserve | $0.05 assumption |
| Refund allowance | 5% × $7 = $0.35 assumption |
| Amount left after these deductions | $7 − $0.955 − $0.05 − $0.35 = **$5.645**, before payout fees, taxes, marketing, fixed costs and founder time |
| Fixed operating base | $10/month illustration; actual domain/tools/bills remain unpriced |

At two orders, 2 × $5.645 − $10 = $1.29 remains before the other costs; at ten orders, 10 × $5.645 − $10 = $46.45. Neither scenario establishes recovery of preparation/startup work. The earlier US-card fee scenario leaves $0.105 more per order. An earlier eight-hour promotional plan with two sales would use four marketing hours per order; neither the sales nor that workload was observed.

The earlier first-test allocation was ₹3,000 within the ₹5,000/month ceiling, leaving ₹2,000 headroom. It was a budget proposal, not a quote or spending authorization. Existing subscriptions/free allowances, asset rights, checkout access and participant access were not established as sufficient.

**Cash dependency:** Approval, the $50 minimum, 13-day hold and twice-monthly payout schedule can delay receipt. The earlier simplified calculation put nine $7 orders above the threshold, subject to refunds and payout conditions. If the founder receives funds in India, the documented bank/verified-PayPal eligibility matters. No approval or payout date is established. [Payout conditions](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#shared-e002).

**Next useful check:** Verify founder/product eligibility and actual tool bills before choosing a checkout. Price the first experiment under ₹5,000/month; do not assume a sale is profit or immediately withdrawable money.
