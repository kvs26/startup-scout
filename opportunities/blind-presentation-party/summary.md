# A party game where you present slides you have never seen

We would sell a downloadable game for friends at a gathering. One person stands up and gives a pretend serious presentation. The host shows slides on a laptop or television, one at a time. The speaker has never seen them and must invent an explanation as each appears. The fun is watching someone try to connect unexpected pictures and statements into a story.

**Imagined example:** Riya volunteers to speak. The first slide says, “My fridge runs a nightclub.” She begins, “The party starts when we switch off the kitchen lights.” The host advances to a picture of milk cartons queuing outside a fridge, with a cucumber guarding the door. Riya now has to explain why the cucumber is the bouncer. The next slide shows a chart where cheese is the most popular dance partner. She invents a reason and finishes her story. After about three minutes, the host ends her turn and invites another friend to present a different unseen set of slides. These are illustrative details, not finished or tested game content.

The proposed buyer is the person organizing the gathering. A suggested **US$7 one-time payment** would buy twelve short presentations, instructions and voting cards for choosing a favorite performance; exact voting rules still need deciding. The host downloads the presentations beforehand and shows them using a simple browser page that works without an internet connection; ordinary slide files or PDFs would provide a backup. Guests do not need accounts or preparation. The formats and price remain proposals.

This would be a small product you own and sell repeatedly, starting with English-speaking adult game-night hosts. North American buyers supplied the initial research and pricing context; an India-specific version and price still need investigation.

## Why someone might pay

The proposed appeal is having an entertaining activity ready for the evening without writing the presentations yourself. There is some evidence for the occasion: one player reported buying a discounted Jackbox game bundle after enjoying this kind of game at a friend's party. But another organizer explicitly wanted free material and rejected AI content. These are individual accounts, not evidence that customers would buy our version. [Purchase account](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e001) · [Contrary account](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-29-fresh-discovery.md#i003-e002).

Free slide collections, KAPOPO and Jackbox's Talking Points already offer ways to play. Our presentations would have to be worth choosing for their content and convenience. Cheap downloads alone are not a proven advantage. Later collections might sell because familiar slides lose their surprise, but repeat buying is untested. [Alternatives and their limits](research.md#c002).

## How customers might find it

The proposed first route is short Instagram or X videos showing a real turn: the unexpected slide, the speaker's response and the group's reaction. Viewers could try one complete free presentation, see the $7 price and buy the full collection.

That requires willing players, filming and editing, and time answering potential buyers. No audience or dependable route to sales is established. A funny clip could attract viewers who never buy. The supplied Instagram carousel and reel were not freshly inspected, so the research does not establish current trends there. [Acquisition assessment](research.md#c004).

## How you would produce and run it

Your intended workflow is for AI to create the product while you direct the work and make decisions. For this idea, that would mean AI proposing presentation topics, writing slide sequences, generating the pictures, arranging readable slides and creating the download/browser files. You would choose a direction and request AI revisions when the sequence is confusing, repetitive or unsuitable.

The difficult part is making presentations ordinary players can enjoy. You would need to learn what gives a speaker enough context to improvise, observe players attempting a short presentation, and turn their confusion into specific revision requests. No such production or play session has happened. We have not established whether AI revisions can reach the required quality, or how many attempts that would take. If success depends on you personally rewriting the jokes or drawing replacement pictures, that would conflict with the intended production workflow.

The practical advantage is that the same completed files can serve many customers. The work still includes testing their display on ordinary laptops/televisions, fixing download problems, handling refunds and finding buyers. Creating fresh collections adds more content work. The earlier short preparation estimate is **unverified** and should not be used as a reliable launch schedule or evidence of quick earnings. [Production assessment and earlier estimate](research.md#c003).

Your recorded limits are **₹5,000 per month and usually 10–19 hours per week**. The AI tools, revision effort and access to willing players have not been priced or confirmed. At $7, an earlier illustration left about $5.65 per sale after selected checkout, delivery and refund deductions, before marketing, founder time and other costs. The examined checkout also delays payouts and requires merchant eligibility checks. Both costs and access to the money matter before selling. [Detailed assumptions and payout limits](research.md#c005).
