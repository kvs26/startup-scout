# Blind dates for household objects

A browser party game for adults who host small gatherings: choose a fictional date from an anonymous profile, then discover it is one of your friends' ordinary possessions. The group supplies the objects that make each reveal personal.

At the beginning of a round, each player privately photographs one object and enters its name and a real quirk. The software uses the name and quirk to draft a short flirtatious biography; the photograph is saved for the reveal. Each person reviews only their own object's wording and can edit it, regenerate once or replace the object. The host sees who is ready, so they can play too. Once everyone is ready, the approved anonymous biographies appear on players' phones, with their own excluded. Everyone chooses one; several people may choose the same profile. All choices lock. The host then reveals each profile's admirers followed by its object photograph, one profile at a time. Unchosen objects are revealed too; the round ends after every object is shown.

**Imagined example:** A friend submits an old toaster that burns one corner. Its profile reads, “Warm, dependable, a little rough around the edges. Looking for someone who can handle my darker side.” You choose it over the other profiles. The reveal shows the battered toaster and that two friends chose it too. You can save an optional card showing the profile and your ridiculous choice. This example illustrates the intended joke; nobody has tested whether it is funny or sufficiently surprising.

The proposed offer is **$7 paid by the host for one private room, up to eight players including the host and three fresh rounds**. Guests join free through a link. Start with one object each; another round needs a different object, photograph and review from everyone. Three rounds is the allowance, not a promise of thirty minutes or proof of value. The first market would be English-speaking adult game-night hosts; the initial content would be non-graphic comedy.

People already buy and enjoy absurd object-romance games such as Date Everything, and bad-date party games such as Red Flags. Object dating itself is old. This proposal combines friends' real possessions, hidden biographies, a choice and a coordinated reveal. A host could imitate it with paper or a group chat for free. The product would need to make preparation easier and the reveal better enough to earn the purchase. No demand for this exact version has been validated. The sharing hypothesis is specific: a short demonstration lets viewers choose a profile before revealing the object. It can lead to a free two-object example and then the paid host room. Players can share their own optional result cards; a proposed interactive version would let outsiders choose before uncovering the photo. The first customer work is to find five to eight people who already organize game nights and see whether the complete experience earns a purchase. No such hosts have been recruited, and neither a willing promotional creator nor a rate of viewers buying is established. An invitation brings another player, not automatically another paying host. You would direct AI-assisted coding of private rooms, submission, choice and reveal screens, and the constrained biography generator. Players supply the photos; no generated pictures or videos are needed. Your main creative work is making profiles intriguing before the reveal and recognizably connected to the object afterward. You must review examples, revise the writing rules, observe novice groups, check phones and interrupted sessions, manage deletion/refunds, and create honest demonstrations. You should not need to rewrite every customer's game by hand; if you do, the low-priced software model fails.
