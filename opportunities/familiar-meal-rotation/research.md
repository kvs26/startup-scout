# Familiar-meal rotation — research handoff

Assessed 2026-10-18. Origin: [meal-decision scouting campaign](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-18-discovery-menu.md), fifty completed assessments. [Current self-contained summary](summary.md).

## Proposition and decision

A household cook enters familiar complete meals, usual sides and simple effort/repetition preferences. The proposed web planner assembles an editable week and preserves and exports their repertoire. Initial price hypothesis: ₹499 once for the current version. Support/update terms remain unsettled; unlimited concierge setup or permanent cloud-service obligations are not assumed.

Scout verdict: **candidate worth investigating; leading recommendation from this campaign**. The user selected the theme of deciding what to eat, not this product. No investigation round, customer result or production artifact exists yet.

The buying-reason hypothesis is less total setup and weekly correction. A feature-gap claim about automatic personal rotation is contradicted by current free products. Any later investigation must preserve that distinction.

## Requirements carried to the investigator

Preserve the household's own meals and complete-dinner outcome. Do not silently broaden the offer into recipes, dieting, grocery inventory or delivery integration. The first geography, language, acquisition channel and exact support terms remain open. Global eligibility does not mean a global launch, and historical overseas payments do not validate the INR price.

The founder reports 10–19 hours/week and about ₹5,000/month, plans AI-assisted production, has limited independent debugging experience and no established food-domain customer access. Startup tools, domain and external review costs are unpriced. Hosting is only one cost; one-time revenue must pay for its support obligations. The fifty-sale illustration and acquisition/setup sensitivities are in the summary and [final campaign decision](../../scouting-runs/2026-10-18-discovery-menu.md#final-decision); none is a forecast.

A potential growth path is trusted creator distribution followed by licensed repertoires and paid household features. Rights, partner shares, repeated purchases and paid collaboration are all untested. This path cannot be assumed when judging the entry product's economics.

## Next decision-changing work

1. Compare the same real household repertoire in a fixed paper rotation, Amamable and OnRotation. Check actual input requirements and correction effort instead of inferring competitor limitations from landing pages.
2. Identify a reachable segment that already keeps a repertoire and still struggles to assemble complete dinners. Reconstruct recent behavior, including occasions when a fixed cycle works well.
3. Determine whether a narrowly specified paid offer earns preference after including setup, weekly corrections and support. Design a real buying test separately from problem interviews; no test or accepted price exists yet.
4. Investigate distribution access and acquisition cost alongside support. The offer weakens if each inexpensive purchase requires lengthy founder setup or costly promotion.
5. Define a small production-quality check for the user's separate implementation workflow if later justified. Scout and investigator research do not themselves create a product.

A result favoring free tools is decision-relevant evidence against the proposed distinction. Repeated private use without paid preference would support utility, not a business. A few enthusiastic comments alone cannot establish retention or customer acquisition.
