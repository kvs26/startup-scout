# Sources and lessons for the idea scout

Reviewed 2026-10-17. This is a design notebook for `startup-idea-scout`, not a list of validated startup opportunities. I reviewed **40 distinct original sources**: 17 agent workflows or repositories, 16 discovery and testing methods, and 7 first-party descriptions of evidence channels. A source's advice is a proposal to examine, not proof that its own method works. The notes below state what to borrow, what to question, and how it applies to our scout.

## Main finding

Our poor results are partly a **discovery problem**, not merely a strict rubric problem. Searching broad themes, turning public complaints into product concepts, and then scoring those concepts can produce plausible but weak ideas. The scout should spend more of its effort understanding one reachable buyer group's real tasks, current substitutes, decision makers, and consequential failures. It should reserve a positive verdict for a *specific opportunity worth testing*, then make the next customer test explicit. When public evidence is insufficient, it should say which fact is missing and how to get it. No framework can guarantee a good idea at the end of a web search.

The current skill already moves in this direction: it starts from a buyer segment and workflow, checks substitutes, distinguishes observed facts from inferences, and has a no-candidate diagnosis. The remaining improvement is **execution discipline**: require a narrow research question and a stop condition for each search pass, inspect original accounts rather than snippets, avoid spreading effort across many unrelated topics, and report what direct observation would change the verdict.

## Agent workflows and idea-related repositories (1–17)

1. [idea-finder](https://github.com/zhenya-vlasov/idea-finder) starts by interviewing the founder about roles, experience, frustrations, and network before researching industries. **Borrow:** access and lived context as search inputs, plus a resumable discovery record. **Question:** a 50-point ranking can make sparse evidence look precise.
2. [idea-hunt](https://github.com/ANVEAI/idea-hunt-skill/blob/main/SKILL.md) mines complaints and existing paid workflows, then asks for proof of willingness to pay before building. **Borrow:** investigate where money is already spent and use explicit rejection reasons. **Question:** its fixed build-time and automation gates, and a demand that *every* idea replace a paid workflow, could exclude ambitious new categories.
3. [app-store-product-researcher](https://github.com/vincent-peng/app-store-product-researcher) goes from a niche to named competitors, recent reviews, and a concrete gap. **Borrow:** inspect a particular product's recent customer complaints before proposing a feature. **Question:** mobile app signals and estimated app revenue do not directly establish B2B buyer demand.
4. [validate-idea-skills](https://github.com/elcodabra/validate-idea-skills) separates audience/problem work, competitor work, smoke tests, presales, and a decision. **Borrow:** keep problem discovery separate from demand experiments. **Question:** its sample and conversion targets should not become universal scout thresholds.
5. [yc-startup-ideation](https://github.com/fslfrd/yc-startup-ideation) distinguishes evidence, inference, and assumption, and has “hold for evidence” as an outcome. **Borrow:** an uncertain lead can be held with a decisive next question rather than accepted or discarded. **Question:** it mainly evaluates supplied ideas; its larger rubric does not itself find a problem.
6. [hypothesis-validator](https://github.com/evgeniy038/hypothesis-validator) seeks a kill, reshape, or pursue decision from a specific customer and money hypothesis. **Borrow:** every research section should affect the next decision. **Question:** it assumes a hypothesis has already been supplied, so it cannot replace open discovery.
7. [awesome-pm-skills: validate-idea](https://github.com/menkesu/awesome-pm-skills/blob/main/skills/validate-idea/SKILL.md) prioritizes risky assumptions and cheap tests with a stated failure condition. **Borrow:** test the assumption most likely to change the decision first. **Question:** thresholds must fit the test and buyer, rather than being copied as fixed numbers.
8. [first-customer-finder](https://github.com/carolinacherry/claude-first-customer-finder-skill) verifies public prospect signals and prepares contact routes without claiming those people are interested. **Borrow:** reachability is part of opportunity quality; distinguish a prospect from an engaged buyer. **Question:** a public signal or prospect score is not evidence of a response or purchase.
9. [Agent Factory discovery skill](https://github.com/panaversity/agentfactory-business-plugins/blob/main/innovation/skills/discovery/SKILL.md) keeps problem interviews, synthesis, and validation at different stages and labels assumption status. **Borrow:** record whether a claim is assumed, anecdotal, or directly tested. **Question:** prescribed counts of insights are formatting rules, not evidence standards.
10. [startup-idea skill](https://github.com/fabricioctelles/skills/blob/main/skills/startup-idea/SKILL.md) treats spreadsheets, messages, staff, and services as competitors and asks who uses versus pays. **Borrow:** map the complete status quo and the payer/user split. **Question:** brand and market-positioning work is premature before a real problem is established.
11. [create-business skill](https://github.com/wondelai/skills/blob/main/create-business/SKILL.md) moves from customer job to interviews, experiments, and strategy with durable artifacts. **Borrow:** stage order and saved evidence. **Question:** a ten-phase process and fixed interview/commitment counts are too heavy for an initial scout.
12. [customer-discovery skill](https://github.com/guia-matthieu/clawfu-skills/blob/main/skills/validation/customer-discovery/SKILL.md) makes business-model assumptions explicit and designs tests that could falsify them. **Borrow:** write what would disprove the opportunity. **Question:** a long canvas can become form filling if underlying customer accounts are thin.
13. [ultimate-product-discovery-skill](https://github.com/ipavelm/ultimate-product-discovery-skill/blob/main/SKILL.md) splits a large discovery process into tasks and loads references when needed. **Borrow:** progressive guidance, so the scout stays usable. **Question:** investor decks and financial plans arrive too early for an unverified buyer problem.
14. [startup-skill release notes](https://github.com/ferdinandobons/startup-skill/releases) describe a preflight scan for incumbents and prior failures, plus customer-anchored versus assumed financials. **Borrow:** inspect substitutes early and label modeled economics. **Question:** release notes describe intended behavior, not measured success of the method.
15. [startup-idea-validator](https://github.com/RiseMore-AI/startup-idea-validator) produces a weighted venture score and can fall back from web research to model knowledge. **Lesson from a counterexample:** numbers and market estimates can create confidence without buyer evidence. A fallback must be disclosed and must not support a positive research verdict.
16. [book-skills: Mom Test](https://github.com/sumitvairagar/book-skills/blob/master/skills/mom-test/SKILL.md) critiques pitches through questions about actual past behavior and present workarounds. **Borrow:** ask about the last incident and what the buyer actually did. **Question:** good interview wording alone does not show budget, purchase authority, or repeat demand.
17. [multi-agent startup-idea-validator](https://github.com/gabrieldejesusrodrigues/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/startup-idea-validator/SKILL.md) combines product, business, and adversarial reviews, with source-quality modifiers and an explicit no-idea outcome. **Borrow:** seek contrary evidence and permit no candidate. **Question:** several agents using the same shallow posts do not create independent evidence; weighted scores and projected revenue can amplify speculation.

## Original discovery and testing methods (18–33)

18. [Paul Graham, How to Get Startup Ideas](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html): notice problems that real people have; a narrow group with intense need can be a starting point if there is a plausible path to adjacent buyers. **Apply:** describe who needs a first version now and how the group could expand. The path beyond the niche can remain a hypothesis.
19. [Y Combinator, How to Pick a Startup Idea](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Ri-how-to-pick-a-startup-idea) ([official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R56RJFZBasQ)): going deep into one customer's business can expose a different, more consequential problem than the starting idea. **Apply:** inspect several tasks inside one segment before jumping across industries. Its advice to commit to building is a founder decision, not a requirement for a research-only scout.
20. [YC Startup School, evaluating ideas and talking to users](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky/): ask about the last occurrence, its difficulty, and attempted solutions; begin with a problem rather than an AI technique. **Apply:** require a concrete incident and current response. A remembered incident is still one account, not market prevalence.
21. [YC Startup School, pricing and conversion](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-7-recap-kevin-hale-on-conversion-rates-and-pricing/): price and conversion must relate to delivered value and a real acquisition path. **Apply:** separate a competitor's published price from a customer's accepted price for our proposed offer. Do not import generic conversion benchmarks.
22. [Paul Graham, Do Things that Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html): manual early work can reveal what customers really need and how they adopt a solution. **Apply:** consider a small founder-assisted learning test where feasible. Manual work is not automatically an acceptable long-term product or compatible with this founder's time.
23. [Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/): the buyer's circumstances and desired progress explain why an alternative is chosen; substitutes may cross product categories. **Apply:** map the trigger, desired outcome, and present workaround before naming a software category.
24. [Product Talk, Discovering Solutions](https://www.producttalk.org/discovering-solutions/): interviews help uncover opportunities; assumption tests help choose between solutions. **Apply:** do not infer the winning feature directly from a complaint. Treat the proposed solution as a later hypothesis.
25. [Strategyzer, Testing Business Ideas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/testing-business-ideas-book): experiments differ in cost, speed, and strength of evidence. **Apply:** choose the smallest test that addresses the most important unknown. The linked page describes a book; its marketing claims are not evidence of a particular opportunity.
26. [Strategyzer, Good Ideas Are Bad for Innovators](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/good-ideas-are-bad-for-innovators): specify a hypothesis, test, measure, and success criterion before an experiment. **Apply:** precommit to what would change the scout's recommendation. A well-written test card cannot substitute for running the test.
27. [Strategyn, Why Innovation Fails](https://strategyn.com/why-innovation-fails/): ask which outcome a customer values and whether current options satisfy it. **Apply:** state the precise underserved outcome, such as fewer exceptions or shorter approval time. Importance and satisfaction scores need actual customer measurement.
28. [GOV.UK, Learning about users and their needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs): learn who the users are, what they need to do, and how they do it now. **Apply:** write a task and actor map, including nonusers affected by the work. Policy and stakeholder opinions do not replace observed user needs.
29. [GOV.UK, How the discovery phase works](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works): frame the problem and study the whole service journey before choosing a solution. **Apply:** inspect handoffs, exceptions, and approval steps beyond the one screen an agent could automate.
30. [GOV.UK, Plan user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service): research should answer prioritized questions with suitable methods. **Apply:** a search pass needs a question and an exit condition; stop repeating searches that cannot answer a private buying question.
31. [Steve Blank, Customer Development Is Not a Focus Group](https://steveblank.com/2009/11/30/customer-development-is-not-a-focus-group/): customer conversations test problem and solution hypotheses; collecting requested features can mislead. **Apply:** use interviews to understand behavior and a purchase test to assess commercial commitment.
32. [Steve Blank, Founders Need to Be Ruthless When Chasing Deals](https://steveblank.com/2024/04/16/founders-need-to-be-ruthless-when-chasing-deals/amp/): an enthusiastic user or champion may lack budget and authority. **Apply:** distinguish user, advocate, payer, approver, budget, and procurement path before assuming a B2B sale.
33. [World Bank Enterprise Surveys methodology](https://www.enterprisesurveys.org/en/methodology): trustworthy prevalence estimates require a defined population and sampling design. **Apply:** treat review sites, forums, and search hits as selected cases. Do not turn “I found five complaints” into “many businesses have this problem.”

## How to use evidence channels (34–40)

34. [G2, How G2 ensures authentic reviews](https://documentation.g2.com/help/docs/how-g2-ensures-authentic-reviews): moderation, reviewer checks, and labels improve context, while G2 also permits AI-assisted reviews under conditions. **Use:** read the full review, reviewer role, product, date, and response; verify the claimed gap elsewhere. One review is a lead, not demand prevalence.
35. [Gartner Peer Insights FAQ](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/faq): reviewer profiles can show role, industry, and organization size; some reviews are solicited or incentivized. **Use:** separate end user from technology purchaser and read “additional context.” Ratings alone do not reveal the workflow or buyer authority.
36. [GitHub documentation, searching issues and pull requests](https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/filtering-and-searching-issues-and-pull-requests?tool=webui): issue state and linked work can show whether a developer-reported gap remains open or was fixed. **Use:** inspect date, replies, resolution, and release state. Feature requests from developers do not prove B2B willingness to pay.
37. [Apple, Customer Reviews API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreconnectapi/customer-reviews): Apple's documented API is for reviews of **your own app**. **Use:** public competitor review pages may generate leads, but first verify lawful, practical access to full competitor reviews; do not assume an API or a review-mining script has that access.
38. [UK Contracts Finder API documentation](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/apidocumentation): notices can represent future work, live tenders, or awards. **Use:** a requirement describes a buyer's stated task; an award offers a stronger spending signal. Check notice stage and supplier eligibility before generalizing to private businesses.
39. [US SAM.gov Opportunities API](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/): procurement notices expose explicit scopes and procurement stages, with access requirements for the API. **Use:** identify exact tasks and buying language; a notice is not evidence that a small private customer will buy the same solution.
40. [EU TED Search API](https://docs.ted.europa.eu/api/latest/search.html): public procurement notices are searchable across EU buyers. **Use:** compare stated requirements and award context where available. Procurement obligations and purchase procedures differ from the founder's likely initial B2B customer.

## Design implications for our skill

1. **Start with a reachable buyer group and its work.** Choose a group the founder can study through written sources and potentially contact later. Map several tasks in that group before proposing an offer. Record why the group was chosen; accessibility is a research condition, not a claim they will respond.
2. **Research one decisive question at a time.** For each pass, state what observation would support or weaken the hypothesis and which original sources could answer it. Keep source authorship, event date, full-text access, relevant replies, and duplicate accounts visible. Search snippets and investor themes are leads only.
3. **Check the present substitute early.** The strongest competitor may be a bundled feature, employee, spreadsheet, service, or doing nothing. A low-priced substitute does not automatically kill an idea; identify a specific consequential gap and who experiences it.
4. **Use evidence states instead of a total score.** A scout can say “worth customer testing,” “promising but blocked on a named observation,” or “no defensible candidate.” Require an explicit payer, value or cost anchor, access route, and feasible first version before the first state. Do not label public research “validated demand.”
5. **If the web runs out, say why and change method.** No readable buyer accounts is an access limit; a solved complaint is a negative market finding; a missing purchase decision is a private information gap. Recommend the smallest authorized next step—usually a focused conversation or commitment test—without treating more generic searches as a cure.
6. **Preserve ambition without pretending it is evidence.** After a narrow first customer and task are understood, describe plausible adjacent customer groups and deeper outcome ownership as hypotheses. Do not discard a potentially large business because its first wedge is small, or inflate a small wedge into a giant market using imagined revenue.

## What this review does not establish

These 40 sources improve how to search and interpret findings. They do **not** identify a buyer with a verified urgent problem for us, establish a price anyone will pay, or show which segment will yield a strong opportunity. Those claims require opportunity-specific original evidence and, for purchase intent, direct customer learning or a live offer. The scout should be judged by the clarity of that evidence and decision, including an honest “no candidate,” rather than whether it always emits an idea.
